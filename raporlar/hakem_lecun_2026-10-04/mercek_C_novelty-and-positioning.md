## Overall

**Recommendation from lens C: Major revision.** No finding in this lens would cause rejection on its own. The paper is unusually honest about prior art in its body (Ravikumar's Eq. 1 is named as the successful-source metric, Mahmood and Ravikumar as the source of both-correct, "Both are established", the TRADES algebra is acknowledged, the ATEEC preliminary version is disclosed and its error corrected). The problems are at the level of framing: the abstract, introduction and conclusion claim more than the body does, in three places (literature-level explanation of the disagreement, "an identity we derive", "pre-registered"), and two specific external claims are checkable and currently either wrong (Mahmood's clean accuracies "would be required") or rest on quarantined data (p = 0.88). All fixes are text edits or desk analyses on published tables and existing per-sample logs; none requires retraining.

The genuinely new, defensible contribution is: on one adversarially trained CNN/ViT pair, three seeds, three datasets, the conditioning set alone moves the transfer asymmetry by 15.01 points against a retraining SD of 0.23 to 1.27 (results/C1_REFERANS_FOYU.md:56-60; 05_discussion.tex:13), the spread decomposes exactly into a closed-form clean-error term (9.21 = 13.57 - 4.36, C1_REFERANS_FOYU.md:53-54), a different-estimand term (5.79) and a residual discretion term (3.91 = 8.27 - 4.36), and on a clean-accuracy-matched pair (SVHN) two protocols reverse the sign. The 9.21 figure is reproduced by the identity: with ViT clean error 1 - 0.7353 and CNN clean error 1 - 0.8579 and the target-correct rates of C1_REFERANS_FOYU.md:54, e_ViT(1 - 0.1987) - e_CNN(1 - 0.1551) = 0.2121 - 0.1201 = 9.20 points. I checked this myself.

## Strengths

1. The positioning against the two closest benchmarks is precise and correct in kind: "TA-Bench holds the scoring rule fixed and varies the attack and the model pool; Yu et al. vary the scoring rule along the target-aggregation axis. We vary it along the conditioning axis instead" (02_related_work.tex:62, p. 4). This is a clean, falsifiable statement of difference.
2. Prior ownership of the protocols is stated, not hidden: "Both are established. The successful-source rate is the transferability metric of Ravikumar et al., whose Equation (1) ..." (03_methodology.tex:118). The paper claims the sweep, not the metrics.
3. The TRADES overlap is acknowledged in the body with the right technical distinction: "The algebra itself is not new. Written over a single model ... is the natural-plus-boundary decomposition of TRADES ... What is new here is that in the transfer setting the premise is not definitional" (04_experiments.tex:145, p. 11). The distinction (p is definitional in TRADES because R_bdy uses an existential quantifier over the ball; it is empirical for a fixed source perturbation) is correct. Note: gatherer C1's statement that the manuscript contains no natural/boundary text (toplama_C1, line 12 and A13) is wrong; I verified the sentence at 04_experiments.tex:145.
4. The direction disagreement with Mahmood, Benz and Shao is surfaced in the introduction (01_introduction.tex:9) and discussed rather than buried (05_discussion.tex:17: "Whether our direction is the correct one is a separate question").
5. The paper corrects its own preliminary version in print (01_introduction.tex:31: "it corrects one claim of the earlier version"), and the claim it corrects is indeed in the ATEEC text ("The direction of the effect is therefore stable across both checkpoint sets", bildiri.tex:148; "its direction is not", bildiri.tex:190).
6. No "first", "novel" or "to our knowledge" claims anywhere (gatherer C1 grep, verified by my own reading of all six sections). The single negative-existence claim ("we know of no controlled estimate", 02_related_work.tex:74) survived gatherer C2's sweep (toplama_C2, Tablo C row 1).
7. IEEE Access fit is real: a direct predecessor appeared there (ali2024adversarial, IEEE Access 12:105281, cited at 02_related_work.tex:72), and the journal reviews for soundness more than for headline novelty, which suits a methodological study with negative results.

## 🟡 Major

**Y1. The abstract's literature-level claim is not tested, and the paper's own discussion contradicts it for the one disagreement it examines.**
What: Abstract sentence 2, "We show that much of the disagreement comes from the measurement protocol" (main.tex:89, p. 1); title "The Measurement Protocol Decides the Conclusion" (main.tex:56). The paper never re-scores a published comparison. The disagreement the introduction cites first, transfer direction (01_introduction.tex:9), is by the paper's own account not a protocol effect: "Under a both-correct protocol comparable to ours, Mahmood et al. report the opposite direction" (05_discussion.tex:17, p. 19). On CIFAR the direction is the same under all four protocols (05_discussion.tex:21); the protocol changes magnitude and the equivalence verdict, and changes sign only on SVHN, and there only for the two protocols the paper itself does not endorse ("though the two we endorse agree", main.tex:89).
Why it matters: a top reviewer reads the abstract against the discussion. "Much of the disagreement" is a claim about the literature; the evidence is a within-study sensitivity on one model pair. This is the single most likely line to draw a "claims exceed evidence" verdict.
Fix: restate the claim at the level actually measured, e.g. "On identical models and data, the choice of conditioning set moves the measured transfer asymmetry by more than ten times what retraining does, and it can decide an equivalence verdict and, when clean accuracies are matched, the sign". Either drop "much of the disagreement comes from" or support it with Y2's re-analysis. Consider softening the title to "... Can Decide the Conclusion" or "Measurement Protocol as a Source of Disagreement in ...".

**Y2. "Testing this properly would require their per-direction clean accuracies" is factually wrong; the data are published and the test can be done now.**
What: 05_discussion.tex:17. I fetched arXiv 2104.02610 (Mahmood, Mahmood, van Dijk) and read it. Section 4.1, Eq. (3) defines transferability over "a set of m examples that are correctly classified by both"; Section 4.2 uses m = 1000 and reports "the highest transferability result" over FGSM, PGD and MIM per pair. Table 1 (CIFAR-10) gives clean accuracy ("Acc"): ViT-L-16 99.1%, BiT-M-R50x1 97.5%, ResNet-56 92.8%, ResNet-164 93.8%; white-box PGD robust accuracy is 0.0 to 1.8% for all models. Table 2 (CIFAR-10, row = source, column = target): ViT-L-16 to BiT-50x1 42.5%, BiT-50x1 to ViT-L-16 5.7% (also quoted in their Sec. 4.3); ViT-L-16 to ResNet-56 13.0%, ResNet-56 to ViT-L-16 5.3%.
Why it matters: (a) the sentence asks the community for data that already exist, which a reviewer who knows Mahmood will notice; (b) the data partly support the paper (in both pairs the target with lower clean accuracy, the CNN, receives more transfer, matching "the weaker target in Mahmood et al.'s pair is not the weaker target in ours"); (c) the data also stress the mechanism: a 1.6-point clean gap goes with a 36.8-point both-correct asymmetry, and under FGSM the ViT-L-16 is the more white-box-vulnerable model (56.3% vs 66.0% robust) yet receives far less transfer, which does not fit "incoming transfer tracks the target's own vulnerability" (01_introduction.tex:25, r = 0.986). Regime differences (standard-trained, ImageNet-21k pre-trained, max over three attacks, 1000-sample subsets) are a legitimate reason the mechanism may not transfer, but that must be said, not deferred.
Fix: replace the sentence with a short paragraph (or a supplementary table) that tests the mechanism on Mahmood Tables 1 and 2 with the caveats above, and qualify "comparable to ours" (their protocol takes the maximum over three attacks per pair and uses standard training). If the result is mixed, say so; it is still a stronger positioning than an untested "compatible".

**Y3. "An identity we derive" in abstract, introduction and conclusion versus "The algebra itself is not new" in the body.**
What: main.tex:89 ("an identity we derive removes 9.21 points in closed form"), 01_introduction.tex:23 ("removed in closed form by an identity we derive"), 06_conclusion.tex:9 ("an identity we derive"), 02_related_work.tex:74 ("turns a known qualitative caveat into a correction"), against 04_experiments.tex:145 ("The algebra itself is not new ... is the natural-plus-boundary decomposition of TRADES"). The relation r_raw = e + r_cond(1 - e) is the law of total probability with the premise P(still wrong | clean-wrong) = 1. The white-box factorization "robust = clean x conditioned survival" (03_methodology.tex:109; 01_introduction.tex:25 "factors exactly"; 05_discussion.tex:43) is the same decomposition and carries no citation at all where it is introduced.
Why it matters: a theory-minded reviewer will call the identity elementary on sight. If the front matter calls it derived and only page 11 concedes it, the reviewer reads the concession as damage control rather than as the paper's position. The real contribution (measuring the premise p, 0.989 to 1.000 over 36 directions, 01_introduction.tex:25; and using the closed form to apportion the 15.01-point spread) is stronger than "we derive" and should be what the front matter says.
Fix: in abstract, introduction and conclusion say "an elementary identity, the transfer analogue of TRADES' natural/boundary split, whose premise we measure" (or similar), cite zhang2019theoretically at 03_methodology.tex:109 for the white-box factorization, and in 02_related_work.tex:74 move the TRADES sentence up so the related-work section, not the results, owns the attribution. Keep "on a premise we measure rather than assume" (main.tex:89), which is the honest novelty.

## Questions a reviewer will ask, with pre-emptive answers

1. "Isn't your identity just the law of total probability?" Yes, and the paper should say so up front: the contribution is measuring the premise p in the transfer setting, where it is not definitional (unlike TRADES), and using the closed form to apportion 9.21 of the 15.01-point spread. (Y3)
