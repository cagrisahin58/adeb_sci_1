## Overall

**Major revision.** The data layer is unusually strong: per-sample logging, three paired runs, explicit units of inference, honest labelling of averaged-endpoint intervals, withdrawn slopes and a pre-registration failure counted as a failure. Almost every printed number traces to a stored artifact. The problems are in the inferential layer built on top of the data. Two headline interpretations do not survive a direct reading of the paper's own numbers: the decomposition "the identity removes 9.21 of the 15.01 points" is a pivot-dependent accounting, not a property of the data, and the "weaker target" reading of the asymmetry is contradicted by the 3x3 matrix, which shows a source effect. Both are text-level fixes. Neither requires a new experiment. If submitted as is, either one gives a statistically literate reviewer grounds to dismiss the abstract's quantitative message or the second contribution.

## 🔴 Fatal as written (each would let a reviewer reject the corresponding claim; both fixable by rewriting)

### R1. The "9.21 removed by the identity, 5.79 a different estimand, 3.91 survives" accounting is incoherent as an attribution of the spread

- **Where:** main.tex:89 (abstract: "The 15.01-point spread has two parts: an identity we derive removes 9.21 points in closed form, and 5.79 points come from a protocol that estimates a different quantity. What survives is 3.91 points"); 01_introduction.tex:23; 04_experiments.tex:117; 06_conclusion.tex:9; fig:protocol_sets caption (04:109) and the figure itself (p.10).
- **What:** The arithmetic is right (protocol means tc 4.36, bc 8.27, raw 13.57, ss 19.37 from c1_transfer_summary.json; 13.57-4.36 = 9.21; 19.37-13.57 = 5.79; 8.27-4.36 = 3.91). The attribution is not. The 9.21 and 5.79 are distances from an arbitrary pivot (raw), and the "surviving" 3.91 interval [tc, bc] lies *inside* the 9.21 interval [tc, raw], as Figure 3's own brackets show. "Removed + removed = total, and something survives" cannot all be true. If one actually performs the two operations the text describes (map raw onto tc via the identity; set ss aside as a different estimand):
  - identity first: spread of {tc, bc, ss} = 19.37-4.36 = **15.01**, so the identity removes **0** points; then excluding ss leaves 3.91 (ss removes 11.10);
  - ss first: spread of {raw, tc, bc} = 9.21 (ss removes 5.79); then the identity leaves 3.91 (identity removes **5.30**).
  The identity removes 0 or 5.30 points of spread depending on order, never 9.21. The same check on CIFAR-100 (e1_transfer_summary.json means: tc 4.96, bc 10.92, ss 17.50, raw 18.53) contradicts 04:117 "the whole 13.58-point spread there is the identity term": dropping raw leaves a spread of 12.54, so the identity removes 1.03 points (or 7.61 if ss is set aside first). Under L2 (e6_l2_transfer_summary.json: tc 2.12, bc 4.07, ss 12.06, raw 13.03) it removes 0.97 or 8.96.
- **Why it matters:** This is the abstract's main quantitative message. A reviewer who looks at Figure 3 sees nested brackets and asks how 3.91 "survives" inside a term that was "removed". Once asked, the answer is that the identity explains the *bias of the raw estimator* (raw overstates tc by 9.21, fully predicted), not a share of the *spread between protocols*. On CIFAR-10 the spread between the three conditioned protocols is 15.01 with or without the identity.
- **Fix (text only):** State three separate facts. (i) The raw estimate exceeds the target-correct estimate by 9.21 points, and Equation (eq:raw_identity) predicts that gap in closed form from the targets' clean errors (true and well supported). (ii) Among the three conditioned protocols the asymmetry spans 15.01 points; 11.10 of this separates successful-source, a different estimand, from the endorsed pair. (iii) The endorsed pair differs by 3.91 points, run-level 95% t interval about [3.1, 4.7] (per-seed bc-tc = 3.68, 3.77, 4.27 from the summary JSON; 3.70, 3.76, 4.27 from masks; df = 2). Redraw the Figure 3 brackets as pairwise distances, not as a partition. Remove "removes 9.21 points" from the abstract, introduction and conclusion, and remove "the whole 13.58-point spread there is the identity term" (04:117).

### R2. The "weaker target" reading of the asymmetry is contradicted by the paper's own 3x3 matrix

- **Where:** 01_introduction.tex:25 ("Incoming transfer also tracks the target's own vulnerability (r = 0.986 across three targets), which reframes the asymmetry as a property of the weaker target"); 04_experiments.tex:172 ("the CNN->ViT advantage is better read as a property of the weaker target than as evidence that CNN-crafted perturbations are intrinsically more transferable"); 05_discussion.tex:17 ("we attribute the asymmetry to the target's own vulnerability rather than to any special strength of CNN-crafted perturbations ... the weaker target in Mahmood et al.'s pair is not the weaker target in ours"); 05:21; 06_conclusion.tex:9; tab:transfer_matrix and fig:transfer_heatmap (p.12).
- **What:** r = 0.986 is reproduced (c3_summary.json `incoming_transfer_vs_own_vulnerability.pearson_r` 0.98621). It does not support the reading. Holding a third model fixed, which is exactly what the WRN was added for:
  - fixed target WRN: ResNet-crafted 12.48 vs ViT-crafted 8.65, so the **ViT is the weaker source** (by 3.8 points);
  - fixed source WRN: into ResNet 21.48 vs into ViT 18.15, so the **ViT is the less vulnerable target** (by 3.3 points).
  An additive source + target model on the six off-diagonal target-correct cells (1 residual df, max |residual| 0.61-0.83 points; `_tmp/mB_srctgt.py` on results/c1_c3/pair{1,2,3}/transfer_matrix.json and results/q1/cifar100/transfer/pair{1,2,3}/transfer_matrix.json) gives:

  | Dataset | Seed pair | tc asymmetry | Source effect (ResNet minus ViT) | Target effect (ViT minus ResNet) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | CIFAR-10 | 1 / 2 / 3 | 4.81 / 4.50 / 3.92 | +5.09 / +5.23 / +5.07 | -1.55 / -1.96 / -2.57 |
  | CIFAR-10 | mean cells | 4.41 | +5.13 | -2.02 |
  | CIFAR-100 | 1 / 2 / 3 | 4.21 / 4.55 / 6.11 | +8.82 / +10.04 / +9.75 | -6.10 / -7.15 / -5.31 |
  | CIFAR-100 | mean cells | 4.96 | +9.54 | -6.19 |

  The asymmetry is a source effect, partly offset by an opposite target effect, in all six fits. Incoming transfer to ResNet and to ViT is nearly equal on CIFAR-10 (18.50 vs 19.04; in seed 3 it is 19.00 vs 18.98) and *reversed* on CIFAR-100 (29.27 vs 27.86, every seed). r = 0.986 is driven by the single WRN point (incoming 10.57, own 25.16). Outgoing transfer correlates with own white-box fooling at r = -0.94 (CIFAR-10) and -0.96 (CIFAR-100): the less robust the source, the worse its examples transfer. That is a source-robustness account, the opposite of the one the paper states.
- **Why it matters:** The claim is listed as a contribution, carries the reconciliation with Mahmood et al. in the discussion, and is repeated in the conclusion. Two cells of Table 4 refute it, and any reviewer who reads the heatmap row by row will see that. The target-correct conditioning makes the ViT look *less* vulnerable as a target, since its correct set is the easier samples, so the paper's own metric works against its reading.
- **Fix (text only, no new runs):** Replace the target-vulnerability reading with what the matrix shows. The direction comes mainly from the ViT being a weak source under this recipe, consistent with an anti-correlation between a source's own robustness and the transferability of its examples. Report the additive decomposition, or simply the two fixed-third-model contrasts, for both datasets. Rewrite 05:17. The Mahmood et al. disagreement is then *not* explained by target weakness and should be stated as open. Keep r = 0.986 only as a descriptive aside, if at all. Optional citation to check and verify before use: Springer, Mitchell and Kenyon, "A Little Robustness Goes a Long Way", NeurIPS 2021 (robust sources give more transferable examples; **UNVERIFIED**, check DOI/arXiv ID first).

## 🟡 Major

### Y1. "3.91 points of discretion" is itself a closed-form, source-side composition term, and the paper's own mechanism says so
- **Where:** main.tex:89; 01:23 ("still more than twice the retraining effect"); 04:117; 06:9 ("3.91 points of genuine discretion").
- **What:** With w the share of target-correct samples that the source also solves, tc = w*bc + (1-w)*r_excl, so tc - bc = (1-w)(r_excl - bc). From the paper's own numbers (04:117, 04:123; a2b_exclusive_subsets.json via B1 E43/E44): ViT->CNN tc - bc = 15.51 - 9.98 = 5.52 (1,518 ResNet-correct/ViT-wrong samples fooled at 41.2%); CNN->ViT tc - bc = 19.87 - 18.25 = 1.62 (292 samples at 59.1%). The 3.91 equals 5.52 - 1.62. It is fixed by subset sizes, which are set by the clean-accuracy gap, and by subset fragility. It is as mechanical as the 9.21 and rises with the gap (controlled-arm three-protocol slope +0.673, e3_iki_kol_fit.json `kollar.A`). Across datasets it is 3.91 / 5.97 / 0.65 (CIFAR-10 / CIFAR-100 / SVHN), tracking the gap. "Discretion" suggests that analysts can pick freely between two estimates of one quantity. They are two estimands, P(fool | T correct) and P(fool | T and S correct). "More than twice the retraining effect" holds against the largest SD (1.27; ratio 3.1) but not against the largest across-seed range (2.41; ratio 1.6).
- **Fix:** Call 3.91 a second composition term (source-side), give its closed form and its run-level CI, and say the reporting requirement is to *name the estimand*, not to choose between noisy estimates. This strengthens the paper: all three gaps then have mechanisms.

### Y2. SVHN: the same -0.35 is counted both as "equivalent" and as "a ViT advantage"; the equivalence is per-sample only and uses the more favourable seed's p

## Questions a reviewer will ask, with pre-emptive answers

1. **"If the identity removes 9.21 points, why does 3.91 survive inside it?"** Answer after the R1 fix: the identity explains the raw estimator's bias (raw - tc = 9.21), not a share of the cross-protocol spread. Among conditioned protocols the spread is 15.01, of which 11.10 is successful-source vs the endorsed pair and 3.91 is the endorsed pair.
2. **"Your own heatmap shows the ViT row is the weakest. Isn't the ViT simply a poor source?"** Yes. Fixed-target and fixed-source contrasts against the WRN, plus the additive fit (source +5.1, target -2.0 on CIFAR-10; +9.5 / -6.2 on CIFAR-100), all point that way. Rewrite R2 and say the Mahmood et al. disagreement remains open.
3. **"Isn't the identity just the law of total probability?"** Its general form is. The empirical content is that p ≈ 1 for transfer perturbations on adversarially trained targets (0.989-1.000), which is not definitional in the transfer setting, unlike TRADES. The value is practical: published raw rates can be converted to target-correct rates with the target's clean error alone, up to e(1-p).
4. **"With n = 3 runs, what is any run-level p worth?"** Little, and the paper says so. Sparsity is called reproducible rather than significant, the ratio CI includes 1, and the claims that carry the paper (range 15 vs ≤2.4; sign 12/12 per dataset) do not need a p-value.
5. **"Is SVHN equivalence at ±1 meaningful with two runs?"** Only for these two checkpoint pairs. Report the maximum p (0.0011) and drop the "ViT advantage" count for the same -0.35 (Y2).
6. **"Why is 3.91 'discretion' if both protocols are defined precisely?"** It is not discretion in the noise sense. It is the difference between two estimands, with a closed form (5.52 - 1.62); the reporting requirement is to name the estimand (Y1).
7. **"Can I reproduce Table 3 from your released per-sample files?"** Yes to two decimals except 19.37 → 19.36 and 15.01 → 15.00 (rounded-seed aggregation), and Table 3 and Table 4 come from different attack runs (Y7). Fix before release.
8. **"Which CIFAR-100 WRN did you use?"** Pang2022Robustness_WRN28_10 from RobustBench, per experiments/c1_c3_transfer_matrix.py:57. It must be named in the paper (Y5).
