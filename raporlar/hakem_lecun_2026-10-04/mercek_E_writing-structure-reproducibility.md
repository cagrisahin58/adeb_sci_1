## Overall

**Major revision** (lens E). The study's core is well built and partly reproducible: the reference sheet regenerates byte-identically from the release, and the four protocol rates of Table III recompute from the per-sample npz files (E2 T2, T3-F3b). The paper around that core has three problems. First, the availability statement promises more than the release delivers, and the URL is not live yet. That is cheap to fix but would be damaging if a reviewer clicked the link. Second, the framing (title, abstract sentence 2, conclusion) claims more than the evidence, and the paper's own body is more careful than its front matter. Third, the paper is roughly 30% longer than its message needs. That length comes from boilerplate, secondary measurements and the same results stated three times. The prose is competent and unusually candid. Its weaknesses are density, meta-commentary and internal cross-talk between sections that were revised separately (Limitations still cites a slope that Experiments withdraws).

Each item is tagged **[Wrong]** (a factual or internal inconsistency that must change) or **[Preference]** (how I would do it; defensible either way).

## Strengths

1. **Negative results and failed predictions are reported plainly.** The paper says "We count this as a failure, since the registration named the CIFAR-10 spread and not the number" (04_experiments.tex:206), withdraws the four-protocol slope (04:123), reports both units of inference when they disagree (04:303, 03:158), and gives three null results with equal prominence (01:27, 06:13).
2. **The central protocol result is reproducible without a GPU.** `build_reference_sheet.py` run inside the snapshot yields a file byte-identical to `results/C1_REFERANS_FOYU.md` (E2 T2). Table III (`tab:transfer_protocols`, 04:95-98) recomputes exactly from `results/c1_transfer/pair*/per_sample_*.npz` (E2 T3-F3b). Per-sample logging is the right design for a protocol paper.
3. **Numbers agree across front matter and tables.** Abstract 4.4/19.4 matches 04:96-99 (4.36, 19.37). 15.01 = 9.21 + 5.79 has its rounding explained (04:117). 3.91 = 8.27 - 4.36. 1.85 = 94.27 - 92.42 (04:220). 0.41 over 36 directions matches 04:143. r = 0.986 matches 04:172. I found no front-matter number that contradicts a table.
4. **Figure 3 (`fig:protocol_sets`, p10) explains the paper's whole argument in one panel**: nested denominators on the left, the asymmetry each protocol yields on the right, and the decomposition brackets. It is the best figure in the paper.
5. **The paper credits prior work for the identity.** The TRADES natural-plus-boundary precedent is credited explicitly (04:145), and the novelty is stated narrowly: in the transfer setting the premise *p* has to be measured.

## 🟡 Major

**Y-1 [Wrong]. The title and abstract claim more than the paper shows about the literature's disagreement.**
- *Where.* The title is "The Measurement Protocol Decides the Conclusion" (p1). The abstract says "We show that much of the disagreement comes from the measurement protocol" (main.tex:89, sentence 2). The conclusion says "The measurement dominates" (06:9).
- *Evidence against.* On CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 the *direction* is protocol-invariant: "What is stable is the direction: CNN-crafted examples transfer better to the ViT than the reverse under all four protocols, in all three seeds" (04:121), and 12/12 measurements on CIFAR-100 (04:204). The protocol changes the conclusion's sign only on SVHN, which has two seed pairs. The paper never re-scores a published study. 05:17 concedes that "Testing this properly would require their per-direction clean accuracies under a shared protocol". So the paper shows that the protocol *can* move a controlled comparison by 15 points. It does not show that the literature's disagreement *does* come from the protocol.
- *Fix.* Abstract sentence 2: "We show that the measurement protocol alone can move a cross-architecture comparison by more than retraining does." Title, for example: "How Much the Scoring Protocol Moves CNN-versus-ViT Robustness Comparisons". Alternatively, earn the current claim by applying Eq. (11) to the published raw rates of one study that reports clean accuracies (see Missing experiments).

**Y-2 [Wrong]. Limitations rests on a result the Experiments section withdraws, and the withdrawal is buried inside the paragraph that first presents the result.**
- *Where.* 05:55: "the successful-source protocol adds a second driver ... that is strong enough to reverse the overall relationship (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer})". That reversal is the four-protocol slope of -0.528. 04:123 states that slope as a finding ("the spread of the four-protocol asymmetry *falls* as the clean-error gap ... widens") and then withdraws it eight sentences later: "We therefore withdraw the four-protocol slope". 04:127 adds that on the pre-registered pool the arms "neither agree nor disagree" (CI [-0.570, +0.469]).
- *Why it matters.* A reader who skims Limitations takes away a claim the authors have already retracted. A reviewer who reads 04:123 linearly first meets a post-hoc result presented as a finding.
- *Fix.* In 05:55, replace the clause with "the successful-source protocol adds a second, source-driven term whose effect on the spread we could not estimate reliably (Section IV-B)". Reorder 04:123-127 so the pre-registered (SVHN-inclusive) fit comes first, then the three-protocol slope that carries the claim, and the post-hoc SVHN-excluded fit last, labelled as such. Better still, move the whole two-arm calibration (04:123-127, about 900 words) to supplementary material and keep two sentences in the main text: the three-protocol slope and the withdrawal.

**Y-3 [Wrong]. The leakage ablation is described as both needed and done, and it is never reported where results are reported.**

## Questions a reviewer will ask, with pre-emptive answers

1. *"Your link is dead and two-thirds of your figures cannot be regenerated. Why should I trust 'every number'?"* Push before submission, cite a Zenodo DOI, ship the aggregators, and narrow main.tex:118 to what `make reproduce` actually rebuilds (R-1).
2. *"On CIFAR the direction never changes with the protocol, so how does the protocol 'decide the conclusion'?"* Answer: it decides the magnitude (4.4-fold), the equivalence verdict and, near clean-accuracy parity (SVHN), the sign. Say that in the title and abstract instead of claiming to explain the literature (Y-1).
3. *"Your Limitations cites a reversal you withdrew; which slope do you stand behind?"* Answer: only the three-protocol slope (+0.470, CI [+0.361, +0.665]; 04:127). Fix 05:55 (Y-2).
4. *"Was there a leakage ablation or not?"* Answer: yes; six trajectories on one split, no detected effect, and it cannot separate split identity from leakage. Report it in IV-I (Y-3).
5. *"SVHN has two seeds and a 1.85-point gap; is 'matched' and 'equivalence accepted' warranted?"* Answer: the equivalence rests on within-run tests and two concordant runs (-0.34, -0.36). Two runs cannot bound run-to-run variation (04:240), so say "near-matched" and put this qualifier in the conclusion (Y-5).
6. *"Why is a 24-page paper about one protocol choice spending six pages on gradients and attention?"* Answer: those are the three negative readings and the two surviving behavioral differences. Keep their tables, move four figures and the geometry and calibration detail to supplementary material (Y-6).
7. *"Isn't the identity just TRADES' decomposition?"* Answer (already at 04:145): the algebra is, but in the transfer setting *p* is empirical and has to be measured. Give this one sentence in the intro so the reviewer does not discover it on p11.
8. *"Is this 'standard adversarial training'? Your ResNet-18 AA of 37.93% is well below typical PGD-AT."* Answer: it is mixed-loss fine-tuning from clean checkpoints (03:83-97). Name it accurately, and note in Limitations that absolute levels are recipe-bound while the protocol effect is measured within a recipe (Minor 10).
