MOTIVATION AND SIGNIFICANCE

Adversarial robustness remains a critical concern for deploying deep learning
in safety-critical applications. While numerous studies compare CNN and ViT
robustness scores, few investigate WHY these architectures behave differently
under attack. This mechanistic understanding is essential for developing
principled defenses rather than ad-hoc solutions.

KEY CONTRIBUTIONS

1. Transfer Attack Asymmetry: We discover that CNN-generated adversarial
   examples transfer to ViTs at 80% efficiency, while ViT-generated examples
   transfer to CNNs at only 51%. This 29% asymmetry has direct implications
   for black-box attack strategies and ensemble defense design.

2. Gradient Characteristics: We identify that CNN gradients are 4.5× more
   sparse (localized sensitivity) while ViT gradients are 2.2× more aligned
   across samples (vulnerability to universal perturbations). These properties
   directly explain the transfer asymmetry.

3. Semantic Drift Mechanism: We provide the first layer-wise analysis of how
   adversarial perturbations propagate through ViT, showing progressive
   semantic degradation with cosine similarity dropping from 0.997 to 0.917
   and 7.86% feature norm reduction in late layers.

4. Design Principles: Based on our mechanistic findings, we propose specific
   recommendations for robust hybrid system design, including limitations of
   CNN-ViT ensembles and potential benefits of CNN feature extraction with
   ViT classification.

NOVELTY AND IMPACT

Unlike prior work that focuses on benchmarking robustness numbers, our
analysis provides actionable understanding of architectural vulnerability
differences. We believe this perspective will guide the research community
toward more principled defense development.

METHODOLOGY RIGOR

- Evaluation using AutoAttack, the gold standard for robustness assessment
- Statistical validation across 3 random seeds showing <0.15% variance
- Comprehensive analysis pipeline covering transfer attacks, gradient
  characteristics, and feature degradation

ETHICAL STATEMENT

This work studies adversarial vulnerabilities to improve deep learning
security. All experiments use publicly available datasets (CIFAR-10) and
standard evaluation protocols. The findings are intended to help defenders
build more robust systems.
