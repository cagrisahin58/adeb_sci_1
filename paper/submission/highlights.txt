Manuscript: The Measurement Protocol Decides the Conclusion: A Methodological
Study of CNN and Vision Transformer Adversarial Robustness Comparisons

Revised 2026-08-25. All figures come from results/C1_REFERANS_FOYU.md
(3 seeds, full test set, eps = 8/255).

1. The CNN leads under AutoAttack on the full test set (37.93 +/- 0.14%
   against 29.14 +/- 0.40%, McNemar p < 1e-84 per run), and a conditional
   decomposition factors that score exactly into clean accuracy and
   conditional susceptibility. Both factors favour the CNN: a 12.3-point
   higher clean accuracy AND a smaller conditional fooling rate (48.58%
   against 55.53% under PGD-10).

2. With models, data, threat model and attack budget all held fixed, the
   measured CNN-to-ViT transfer asymmetry ranges from +4.4 to +19.4 points
   across four established conditioning protocols: a mean per-seed spread of
   15.01 +/- 0.84 points.

3. Retraining both architectures from scratch moves that same quantity by a
   standard deviation of only 0.23 to 1.27 points. The measurement choice is
   an order of magnitude larger than the training effect the field reports.

4. The confound in unconditioned transfer rates is an identity, not a
   tendency: the deviation from the conditioned rate equals the target's
   clean error scaled by one minus that rate, holding to within 0.41 points
   across 36 measured directions.

5. On SVHN, where the same recipe leaves the architectures only 1.85 points
   apart in clean accuracy, two protocols report a CNN advantage and two a
   ViT advantage. Once the models are matched, the protocol decides the sign.

6. Three claims common in this area fail to replicate: sparsity differences
   do not correspond to spatial localization (3 of 4 measures null), CLS
   attention entropy does not change measurably under attack (equivalence
   accepted in 12 of 12 layers), and a ViT-specific transfer attack gives no
   gain over plain MI-FGSM at a matched budget.
