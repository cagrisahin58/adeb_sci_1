Manuscript: Less a Weak Source than a Different One: Vulnerability,
Similarity and Direction in CNN-ViT Adversarial Transfer under an
Architecture-by-Initialization Design

Log-scale quantities; brackets are 90% intervals over training runs,
conditional on the test images and the panel.

1. Against a fixed panel of seven robust CNNs, a share of 0.936
   [0.925, 0.948] of ViT-Tiny's CIFAR-10 source gap relative to ResNet-18
   lies in symmetric dissimilarity, and the ViT's directional term
   (+0.012) is equivalent to zero within the registered bound of +/-0.10.

2. In a pre-registered architecture-by-initialization design, the
   scratch-trained ViT is less similar to the panel than a ResNet-18 on
   the same input pipeline (-0.364 [-0.415, -0.314]), a contrast that
   moves with the ViT's lower clean accuracy; ImageNet initialization
   narrows it more for the ViT (+0.260).

3. On CIFAR-100 the symmetric share replicates (0.840), but equivalence of
   the ViT's directional term is refuted (+0.143). On the logit scale the
   directional term is strongly negative, so a small directional term is
   limited to CIFAR-10, the log scale and a CNN panel.

4. The gap is retained under the best of PGD-10, momentum PGD and an
   adapted token gradient regularization (0.960), AutoAttack flags none
   of the new models for masking, and on ImageNetV2 transformers are less
   similar to the panel in a descriptive, untested comparison (-0.142).

5. On the same models the conditioning protocol moves the transfer
   asymmetry from +4.36 to +19.37 points (mean per-pair spread
   15.01 +/- 0.84), against run-to-run standard deviations of 0.23 to
   1.27 points; in absolute terms the CNN leads under AutoAttack
   (37.93 +/- 0.14% against 29.14 +/- 0.40%).
