\begin{abstract}
How convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) differ under adversarial perturbations remains an open question. This paper presents a behavioral comparison of the two architecture families under matched standard adversarial training, pairing robustness scores with gradient- and feature-level analyses. Using CIFAR-10 with adversarially trained ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny models evaluated under AutoAttack at the standard perturbation budget of 8/255 on the full test set, we report four findings. First, the CNN attains significantly higher robust accuracy than the ViT (35.74 percent versus 32.94 percent; McNemar paired test on 10{,}000 samples, p below 0.001), a modest but reliable 2.8-point gap. Second, cross-architecture transfer between this model pair is symmetric once each target's clean error is controlled for (conditioned fooling rates of 20.95 versus 20.32 percent, statistically equivalent within a two-point margin), whereas the widely used raw transfer metric manufactures an apparent 8.3-point asymmetry---a methodological caution for transfer studies comparing targets of unequal clean accuracy. Third, scale-invariant sparsity measures show CNN gradients concentrate sensitivity more locally (Hoyer sparsity 0.474 versus 0.449), a difference that persists in a native-resolution ViT control and is therefore not an input-upsampling artifact, while cross-sample gradient alignment is low for both models but higher for the ViT (0.052 versus 0.038). Fourth, ViT feature representations under attack exhibit an early-drop, mid-network-plateau degradation profile, with clean--adversarial cosine similarity falling from 0.994 to 0.955 by mid-network while feature norms change by less than 1.5 percent. These characterizations inform the evaluation and deployment of mixed-architecture ensembles.
\end{abstract}
