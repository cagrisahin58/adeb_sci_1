% ABSTRACT
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Comparisons of adversarial robustness across architecture families report conflicting conclusions, and the disagreement is usually attributed to models, training recipes, or datasets. We show that a substantial part of it comes from the measurement protocol itself. Using adversarially trained ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny pairs, three runs per architecture, full-test-set evaluation with per-sample logging, and a validation split held out before any training, we hold models, data, threat model and attack budget fixed and vary only how robustness and transfer are scored. Across four established conditioning protocols the measured CNN-to-ViT transfer asymmetry ranges from 4.4 to 14.6 points on CIFAR-10, whereas retraining both architectures from scratch moves the same quantity by under 1.5 points, and the protocol decides whether an equivalence test is accepted or rejected. Replicating the design on CIFAR-100 widens the spread and preserves the sign in all twelve measurements. On SVHN, where the same recipe leaves the architectures only 1.85 points apart in clean accuracy, two protocols report a CNN advantage and two a ViT advantage: once the models are matched, the measurement choice decides not only the size of the asymmetry but its sign. A three-architecture control shows that the deviation of unconditioned from conditioned rates is algebraically equal to the target's clean error, not merely correlated with it, so raw rates are contaminated by target weakness by construction. Holding the training trajectory fixed and varying only the checkpoint-selection rule adds a fourth, almost never reported, source of variance. Three claims common in this area also fail to replicate. We close with reporting requirements for cross-architecture robustness studies.
\end{abstract}

The experiments in this study use the CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} and the SVHN dataset~\cite{netzer2011reading}, all of which are publicly available. Pre-trained WideResNet-28-10 weights were obtained from the RobustBench benchmark~\cite{croce2021robustbench, gowal2020uncovering}.
