\begin{abstract}
Understanding why convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) exhibit fundamentally different behaviors under adversarial perturbations remains an open question. While ViTs have achieved remarkable success on clean data, their vulnerability to adversarial attacks compared to CNNs requires deeper investigation. This paper presents a comparative analysis examining \textit{why} these architectures exhibit different adversarial behaviors rather than merely comparing robustness scores. Using CIFAR-10 with adversarially trained ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny models evaluated under AutoAttack ($\epsilon=8/255$), we demonstrate four key findings: (1) CNNs achieve 36.0\% robust accuracy compared to 32.4\% for ViTs, a gap that persists across attack types; (2) Transfer attacks exhibit a consistent asymmetry---CNN-generated adversarial examples cause 41.2\% misclassification on ViT while ViT-to-CNN transfers reach 36.1\%; (3) Gradient analysis reveals CNN gradients are 1.7$\times$ more sparse while cross-sample alignment is comparable across architectures, suggesting that gradient sparsity rather than alignment drives the transfer asymmetry; (4) Feature degradation in ViT increases progressively through transformer layers, with cosine similarity dropping from 0.995 to 0.958 in late layers. These findings provide mechanistic insights into architectural vulnerability differences and suggest design principles for robust hybrid systems.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and Code Availability}
The experiments in this study use the CIFAR-10 dataset~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning}, which is publicly available at \url{https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~kriz/cifar.html}. Pre-trained WideResNet-28-10 weights were obtained from the RobustBench benchmark~\cite{croce2021robustbench}. The source code for the analysis pipeline and trained model checkpoints will be made publicly available upon acceptance.
