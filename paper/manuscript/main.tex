\begin{abstract}
How convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs) differ under adversarial perturbations remains an open question. This paper presents a behavioral comparison of the two architecture families under a matched adversarial objective and threat model, pairing robustness scores with gradient- and feature-level analyses. Using CIFAR-10 with adversarially trained ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny models evaluated under AutoAttack at the standard budget of 8/255 on the full test set, we report four findings. First, the CNN attains higher absolute robust accuracy (35.74 versus 32.94 percent; McNemar p below 0.001), but a conditional decomposition shows this headline is composition-dependent: the PGD-10 gap traces entirely to the clean-accuracy gap (conditioned fooling 52.2 versus 52.3 percent), under AutoAttack the per-model conditional ordering slightly favors the ViT, yet on the jointly solved subset the CNN retains a significant edge under both attacks. Second, the apparent cross-architecture transfer asymmetry depends strongly on the conditioning protocol: raw rates manufacture an 8.3-point asymmetry from unequal clean accuracy, target-conditioned rates are statistically equivalent, while both-correct and successful-source protocols reveal a significant 5.3-point CNN-to-ViT advantage---so protocol choice can create, erase, or reverse directional conclusions. Third, scale-invariant sparsity measures show CNN gradients concentrate sensitivity more strongly (Hoyer 0.474 versus 0.449, paired tests p below 0.001), while cross-sample gradient alignment is higher for the ViT in absolute cosine (0.052 versus 0.038) but near zero in signed cosine for both. Fourth, ViT features degrade with an early-drop, mid-network-plateau profile, unlike the CNN's monotonic depth profile. These results argue for protocol-explicit, multi-view robustness comparisons.
\end{abstract}
