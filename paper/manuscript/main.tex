% ABSTRACT
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Comparisons of adversarial robustness across architecture families reach conflicting conclusions, usually attributed to models, training recipes, or datasets. We show that much of it comes from the measurement protocol itself. On adversarially trained ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny pairs, three runs each, with full-test-set per-sample logging and a validation split held out before training, we fix models, data, threat model and attack budget and vary only how transfer is scored. Across four conditioning protocols the measured CNN-to-ViT transfer asymmetry ranges from 4.4 to 19.4 points on CIFAR-10, whereas retraining both architectures moves it by under 1.5 points. That 15.01-point spread is not one thing: an identity we derive removes 9.21 points of it in closed form, and 5.79 points come from a protocol that estimates a different quantity. What survives is 3.91 points of discretion between the two protocols we endorse. The identity equates the deviation of unconditioned from conditioned rates with the target's clean error scaled by one minus the conditioned rate, on a premise we measure rather than assume. Replicating on CIFAR-100 preserves the sign in all twelve measurements. On SVHN, where the same recipe leaves the architectures 1.85 points apart in clean accuracy, the protocol decides the sign as well as the size: two report a CNN advantage and two a ViT advantage, though the two we endorse agree. Varying only the checkpoint-selection rule adds a fourth, rarely reported, source of variance. Three further readings fail to replicate, and we close with reporting requirements for cross-architecture robustness studies.
\end{abstract}

The experiments in this study use the CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 datasets~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} and the SVHN dataset~\cite{netzer2011reading}, all of which are publicly available. Pre-trained WideResNet-28-10 weights were obtained from the RobustBench benchmark~\cite{croce2021robustbench, gowal2020uncovering}. Upon acceptance we will publish the analysis pipeline, the scripts that generate every table and figure in this paper, the per-sample evaluation records, the fixed validation-split indices and the seed lists, which together reproduce every number reported here; the four conditioning protocols are all computed from the per-sample records, so the protocol comparison can be repeated without retraining. An anonymized copy of that archive is available to reviewers during review at \texttt{[ANONYMOUS-ARCHIVE-URL]}, so the protocol comparison can be checked before acceptance rather than after it. Trained checkpoints total about $40$~GB and are available from the authors on request.
