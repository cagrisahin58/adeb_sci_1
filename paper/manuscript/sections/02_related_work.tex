% 2.1 CNN and ViT robustness

\subsection{CNN and ViT Robustness}
\label{subsec:rw_robustness}

Adversarial training~\cite{madry2018towards} remains the reference empirical defense, and the RobustBench leaderboard~\cite{croce2021robustbench} tracks it across architectures, with wide residual networks at the top of its CIFAR-10 ranking. Within CNNs, skip connections are known to affect transferability~\cite{wu2020skip}, and adversarial training at scale induces batch-normalization statistics distinct from those of clean training~\cite{xie2020intriguing}.

Vision Transformers~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image}, built on self-attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention}, form internal representations that differ measurably from those of CNNs~\cite{raghu2021vision}. Whether this makes them more robust has been answered both ways. Bhojanapalli et al.~\cite{bhojanapalli2021understanding} found ViTs at least as robust as ResNets when pre-trained on enough data, and Shao et al.~\cite{shao2022adversarial} found them more robust in white-box and transfer settings, with features that carry less high-frequency content; Benz et al.~\cite{benz2021adversarial} tied an early comparison of the same kind to frequency preferences. Bai et al.~\cite{bai2021transformers} trained both families under matched recipes and found transformers no more adversarially robust than CNNs, although more robust under distribution shift.

Much of the disagreement concerns training. Mo et al.~\cite{mo2022adversarial} report that adversarial training of ViTs depends on pre-training, which makes a ViT trained adversarially from scratch a known weak point, and Debenedetti et al.~\cite{debenedetti2023light} give a lighter recipe for robust XCiT models. On ImageNet, Singh et al.~\cite{singh2023revisiting} show that a convolutional stem and the training scheme change ViT robustness considerably and release ViT and ConvNeXt models trained with the same recipe, which we use as matched pairs. Jain et al.~\cite{jain2024towards} trace a form of gradient masking in ViTs to softmax underflow in attention, a caveat for PGD-only evaluations, and with diffusion-augmented adversarial training ViTs have been reported to surpass wide ResNets on small datasets~\cite{wu2025vision}. Comparisons that hold the recipe fixed have also been urged on general grounds~\cite{pinto2022impartial}. A recent study in this journal compares ViT and CNN adversarial robustness directly~\cite{ali2024adversarial}; the comparison here differs in that it asks how the two families behave as sources and targets of transfer against a fixed panel, with architecture and initialization varied separately.

\subsection{What Makes a Source and a Target}
\label{subsec:rw_transfer}

Adversarial examples transfer between models, which makes black-box attacks possible~\cite{papernot2016transferability}. Demontis et al.~\cite{demontis2019adversarial} trace transferability to the intrinsic vulnerability of the target, a term that does not depend on the surrogate, and to the alignment between the input gradients of surrogate and target, a term that is symmetric in the two models, alongside the complexity of the surrogate. Adam et al.~\cite{adam2019reducing} attribute the asymmetry between two models of the same architecture to the difference in their gradient norms. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2024why} define gradient similarity between two models on the same input and explain transferability as a trade-off between that similarity and model smoothness, which accounts for the observation that slightly robust surrogates transfer well~\cite{springer2021little}. Awad et al.~\cite{awad2025defense} report, for a zoo of 36 CNNs and ViTs evaluated on ImageNet, that adversarially trained surrogates transfer better than standard ones in all four directions between the families.

A second line asks whether ViT sources are weak because of the attack. Naseer et al.~\cite{naseer2022improving} argue that the poor transfer of conventional attacks crafted on ViTs comes from suboptimal attack procedures and improve it with self-ensembles and token refinement; Wei et al.~\cite{wei2022towards} bypass attention gradients, Token Gradient Regularization (TGR)~\cite{zhang2023tgr} suppresses extreme token gradients, Momentum Integrated Gradients~\cite{ma2023transferable} improves transfer across both families, and Chen et al.~\cite{chen2023understanding} exploit the different inductive biases of CNNs and ViTs. On the measurement side, Mahmood et al.~\cite{mahmood2021robustness} report low transfer between CNNs and ViTs under a both-correct protocol, Shao et al.~\cite{shao2022adversarial} find ImageNet-21k pre-trained ViTs to be strong sources against CNNs, Liu et al.~\cite{liu2023comprehensive} give a source-by-target heat map among adversarially trained ImageNet models, and PubDef~\cite{sitawarin2024pubdef} records transfer across 24 source models. Each of these reports transfer rates or their aggregates. In a search of the transfer literature from 2017 to October 2026 we did not find a study that separates a transfer rate into the target's white-box vulnerability, a similarity shared by both directions and a directional remainder, which is the separation this paper makes. That separation is a parameter-free identity on measured rates whose target term is fixed at a measured value, and a surrogate-complexity term has no separate slot in it: a source main effect enters the directional remainder as a potential and the shared similarity with half weight (Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability}).

\subsection{Asymmetric Matrices and Their Decompositions}
\label{subsec:rw_decomposition}

A transfer matrix is a square table of rates indexed by source and target, and the statistics of such tables is old. Caussinus~\cite{caussinus1965contribution} introduced the quasi-symmetry model for square contingency tables, $\pi_{ij} = a_i b_j \psi_{ij}$ with $\psi$ symmetric. The form used in Section~\ref{subsec:decomposition_def}, $T_{ij} = w_j \simt_{ij}$ before the directional residual is added, is that model with the row effect fixed at one and the column effect replaced by a measured quantity, the target's white-box vulnerability. The antisymmetric part of a pairwise matrix splits orthogonally into a potential, a difference of model scores, and a circulation; this is the combinatorial Hodge decomposition behind HodgeRank~\cite{jiang2011statistical}, and Balduzzi et al.~\cite{balduzzi2018reevaluating} use it to show when Elo-type ratings are adequate for machine-learning evaluation matrices. Its degree-of-freedom count is what makes a two-model asymmetry uninformative about sources and targets (Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability}). Correspondence between two distance matrices over the same objects is tested with the Mantel permutation test~\cite{mantel1967detection}, and Smouse et al.~\cite{smouse1986multiple} extend it to partial correlation given further matrices, the idea behind the residual statistic for gradient alignment in Section~\ref{subsec:gradcos_def}.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocols for Transfer}
\label{subsec:rw_protocols}

How robustness is measured often decides what is concluded. Carlini et al.~\cite{carlini2019evaluating} catalog the failure modes of robustness evaluation, from gradient masking to attacks that are not run to convergence, the concern that the Carlini--Wagner attack~\cite{carlini2017towards} and AutoAttack~\cite{croce2020reliable} address from the attack side, and Tram\`er et al.~\cite{tramer2020adaptive} show that defenses judged robust under fixed attacks fall to adaptive ones. Those works concern attack strength. A second choice, the set of samples a transfer rate is conditioned on, has received less attention. Foundational transfer studies already restrict evaluation to correctly classified samples~\cite{liu2017delving}, attack papers condition their success rates likewise~\cite{dong2018boosting}, and systematic re-evaluations of transfer attacks restrict the pool to samples every model classifies correctly~\cite{zhao2025revisiting}; Mahmood et al.~\cite{mahmood2021robustness} and Ravikumar et al.~\cite{ravikumar2023trend} condition on samples both models classify correctly to remove differences in clean accuracy. Each study states its set and proceeds with it. TA-Bench~\cite{li2023tabench} fixes the scoring rule and varies the attack and the model pool, Yu et al.~\cite{yu2025reliable} vary how an outcome over several targets is aggregated and show that transferability is overestimated when it is measured within one architecture family, and Waseda et al.~\cite{waseda2023closer} separate transfers that reproduce the source's error from those that fail differently. The protocol layer of this paper varies the conditioning set on fixed models instead. Over a single model, the relation between the unconditioned and the conditioned rate is the natural-plus-boundary decomposition of TRADES~\cite{zhang2019theoretically}; in transfer its premise becomes an empirical quantity, which Section~\ref{subsec:metrics} defines.
