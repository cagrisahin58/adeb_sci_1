\subsection{What the Measurements Show}
\label{subsec:findings}

% FOY:M10.2:1839
Against a fixed panel of robust CNNs, the adversarially trained ViT-Tiny looks like a weak source mostly because it is unlike the panel, and the unlikeness runs both ways: once each target's white-box vulnerability is divided out, panel examples transfer into the ViT about as poorly as its own examples transfer out. The dissimilarity is present when both architectures are trained from scratch on a matched pipeline, and ImageNet initialization removes more of it for the ViT than for the ResNet. The directional term, the part that would make a model a weak source in its own right, is small and independent of architecture only on the log scale, against the CNN panel and on CIFAR-10. On CIFAR-100 the ViT's directional term grows to $+0.143$ while the symmetric share replicates, and on the logit scale the ViT's directional term is large and negative, which makes it the directionally stronger source (Section~\ref{subsec:scale}).

The weak-source and dissimilarity readings make different predictions. A source that is weak for its own reasons should gain from a stronger or an architecture-specific attack, yet the gap between ResNet and ViT sources is retained under each source's best attack, and token gradient regularization transfers worse than PGD-10 from every ViT source (Section~\ref{subsec:attack_side}). Similarity to the target family then accounts for most of a source's transfer deficit into it, which fits the report that robust surrogates transfer better~\cite{awad2025defense}. A black-box score measured on a ViT with CNN surrogates therefore reflects dissimilarity along with vulnerability, and the surrogate family belongs in the report. The decomposition separates the two factors that Demontis et al.~\cite{demontis2019adversarial} call target vulnerability and gradient alignment, and Section~\ref{subsec:gradcos} links the similarity term to input-gradient alignment as defined by Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2024why}, a necessary condition that leaves the mechanism open.

Whether the scratch-trained dissimilarity persists at ImageNet scale is unknown. On ImageNetV2 the transformers of the two same-recipe pairs initialized from clean models are, descriptively, less similar to the panel, consistent with the registered expectation, while the single scratch-trained pair is uncertain (Section~\ref{subsec:imagenet}). Nor is it known which component carries the dissimilarity, since the contrast changes patch stem, attention and parameter count together. Three behavioral readings that such comparisons invite failed direct measurement (Appendix~\ref{app:behavior}), and Section~\ref{subsec:future} outlines designs for the open questions.

\subsection{Relation to Earlier Reports}
\label{subsec:prior}

% FOY:M9.9:1708
% FOY:M9.5:1588
An additive fit with the WideResNet reference places the pair's asymmetry on the source (Section~\ref{subsec:pair_decomposition}). Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} explains why two models cannot support that reading. The antisymmetric part of a two-model matrix carries no circulation, and with a single ViT in the family design the within-family similarity loads jointly onto the ViT's source and target effects, so dissimilarity is booked as a weak source. The split becomes identified only once the pair is crossed with a fixed panel and each rate is factored into $w$, $\simt$ and $r$, and then most of the ViT's source deficit is symmetric. Within the pair, about half of the log asymmetry is the ViT's higher white-box vulnerability and the rest is a seed-stable directional residual, which belongs to this pair: against the panel the ViT's directional term is equivalent to zero on the log scale.

Mahmood et al.~\cite{mahmood2021robustness} report the opposite direction, as do Benz et al.~\cite{benz2021adversarial} and Shao et al.~\cite{shao2022adversarial}. The conditioning protocol does not explain the disagreement on CIFAR-10: rescored with Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity}, their BiT-M-R50x1 and ViT-L-16 pair moves by $-0.59$ points under unconditioned scoring, against a both-correct asymmetry of $-36.80$. Their diagonal reports transfer to an independently trained copy of each model, so $w$ is undefined and the decomposition cannot be applied. Our cells suggest one candidate for the reversal. Their models are pre-trained at scale, and in our design ImageNet initialization both moves the ViT toward the CNN panel and gives it a negative directional term. Whether pre-training, scale or both reverse the direction remains open.

\subsection{Reporting Requirements}
\label{subsec:implications}

The four conditioning protocols estimate different quantities, so their distance measures the ambiguity a reader faces when a transfer number arrives without its conditioning set, and that set is the first thing to report (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}). Unconditioned rates should not serve as architecture comparisons, because their departure from the target-correct rate is the target's clean error entering through the law of total probability (Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity}). From published raw rates and clean errors, Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity} bounds the target-correct rate, and on the scale $h=-\log(1-T)$ the two protocols differ only by a target potential (Proposition~\ref{prop:hscale}). A claim about direction needs the targets' white-box vulnerabilities and a fixed panel over which $w$, $\simt$ and $r$ can be separated, with the scale named, because the sign of the directional term depended on it here (Section~\ref{subsec:findings}). Clean accuracies and the checkpoint-selection rule belong in the same report (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}). Initialization has to be kept apart from architecture: by the interaction of Section~\ref{subsec:twobytwo}, an ImageNet-initialized ViT set against a scratch-trained CNN mixes two effects.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

% FOY:M9.4:1553
% FOY:M9.2:1496,1497,1498,1499,1505,1506,1507,1508,1509,1510,1511,1512,1513
Every architectural conclusion rests on two small models, a ResNet-18 and a ViT-Tiny on $32\times32$ images upsampled to $224\times224$, with three runs in the original ViT cell and two in each new cell. Under a Welch correction the degrees of freedom fall to $1.10$ to $1.95$, although the $95\%$ interval of the architecture contrast, $-0.569$ to $-0.160$, still excludes zero. ViT adversarial training is also recipe-sensitive on small datasets~\cite{mo2022adversarial, debenedetti2023light}. The scratch-trained ViT reaches $73.01$ to $74.10\%$ clean accuracy against $83.39$ to $86.35\%$ in the other cells and is more vulnerable. Because the architecture contrast moves with this gap by design, a less converged solution and an architectural dissimilarity cannot be told apart. The ImageNet-initialized ViT comes close to the ResNet cells in clean accuracy and still stays less similar to the panel, but that comparison is not a registered test.

% FOY:M9.4:1551
% FOY:M9.5:1603,1606
% FOY:M9.2:1497
% FOY:M10.2:1839,1857
% FOY:M10.3:1876,1877,1878
% FOY:M9.9:1759
Beyond its dependence on scale and dataset, the directional term is about $+0.20$ for the scratch ViT against the secondary XCiT panel, and its mean of $-0.118$ in the ImageNet-initialized ViT cell lies outside the margin. Equivalence of the architecture main effect therefore does not mean a small directional term in every cell. The initialization factor bundles pretrained weights, clean-phase length and input normalization, whose separate effects, and their interaction with architecture, are not identified. On CIFAR-100 the ViT's clean accuracy of $41.94$ to $44.06\%$ lies below the panel's, so its quantities there may be confounded with accuracy, and the both-correct sensitivity that would bound this was not produced. TGR ran with its original settings in adapted form, with step sizes that differ between attacks by design. The simplest law the decomposition suggests, log asymmetry equal to the log vulnerability ratio, failed along the training trajectories ($R^2 = 0.201$ at unit slope) and is not claimed. The decomposition is an accounting identity, close to the quasi-symmetry model of Caussinus~\cite{caussinus1965contribution} and to the framework of Demontis et al.; its content lies in the measured magnitudes and in their response to intervention.

% FOY:M10.1:1818
% KAYNAK: CLAUDE.md (eps = 8/255)
Our training covers three datasets; ImageNetV2 enters only through independently trained checkpoints, and that panel is descriptive. Its initialized row rests on two pairs of Singh et al.~\cite{singh2023revisiting} and its scratch row on one pair of Liu et al.~\cite{liu2023comprehensive}, counted as scratch-trained on indirect evidence. The ViT-B of Mo et al.~\cite{mo2022adversarial} has a short adversarial schedule and no partner, panel initializations were not verified, and the architectures (convolution-stem ViTs, ConvNeXt, Swin) differ from ours. The common preprocessing departs from some models' own pipeline by at most $1.20$ points of clean accuracy, and the distribution shift of ImageNetV2 enters both $w$ and $T$. The gradient cosine is measured at one clean point, and the partial statistic removes only additive main effects, not the block structure of the two families. An energy-weighted cosine can also hide alignment: for one pair of ImageNet ResNets, recomputation traced a near-zero cosine to aligned low frequencies cancelled by an opposed highest-frequency band. Lacking white-box rates, the matrices of Mahmood et al. stay outside the decomposition (Section~\ref{subsec:prior}). Every model is trained under $\Linf$ at $\eps = 8/255$, so the $L_2$ result probes the protocol and not $L_2$ robustness. The mechanism account of the protocol spread is incomplete: the composition of marginal subsets covers three of the four protocols, and the successful-source protocol adds a second driver tied to the source's own robustness. Our leakage ablation, finally, answers a weaker question than the one posed, since split identity and leakage are not separable in its design.

\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{subsec:future}

A two-by-two design at the next size, ViT-Small against ResNet-50 under one recipe, would show whether the dissimilarity and its response to initialization belong to the families or to these small models; Section~\ref{subsec:computational} gives the cost of the present cells. At ImageNet scale, same-recipe pairs trained from scratch would replace the single indirect pair. A cell that applies ImageNet normalization to random weights would separate normalization from pretraining. Measuring gradient alignment along the PGD path and per frequency band would test whether the clean point stands in for the path the attack travels.
