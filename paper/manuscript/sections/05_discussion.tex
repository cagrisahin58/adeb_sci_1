\subsection{What the Measurements Show}
\label{subsec:findings}

% REF: Tablo III (15.01 +- 0.84); Secim kuralinin asimetriye etkisi, B (kosumlar arasi aralik en cok 2.41)
The central result is an ordering of dispersions on a single quantity. Changing only the conditioning set moves the measured transfer asymmetry by $15.01\pm0.84$ points on fixed models, whereas retraining both architectures moves it across a range of at most $2.41$ points, with standard deviations of $0.23$ to $1.27$ (Section~\ref{subsec:statistics_results}). For scale on a different quantity, re-running an attack with a new random initialization moves absolute robust accuracy by about $0.05$ points and retraining moves the adversarially trained pair by at most $0.55$. A number that the measurement choice moves this far, reported without its protocol, tells a reader little about the architectures.

One objection deserves an answer in its own terms: the four protocols estimate four different quantities, so the distance between them is not a variance, and calling it one would be a category error. We agree, and we make no assumption that the architectures have a single true asymmetry that the protocols estimate with error. Our claim concerns what a reader can recover. When a study reports one number without naming the protocol, the reader cannot tell which of the four quantities was estimated, and the spread we measure is the size of that ambiguity, a bound on what the omission costs. Conditioning itself remains established practice~\cite{liu2017delving, dong2018boosting, ravikumar2023trend} and is necessary; the point is that the choice of conditioning set has to be reported with the result, in the main text.

Whether the direction generalizes is a separate question, and the comparison with Mahmood et al.~\cite{mahmood2021robustness} answers it only in part. They report the opposite direction, transfer out of the transformer stronger than transfer into it, and Benz et al.~\cite{benz2021adversarial} and Shao et al.~\cite{shao2022adversarial} report the same ordering on their own model sets. The protocol does not explain the disagreement on CIFAR-10. Rescored with Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity}, their BiT-M-R50x1 and ViT-L-16 pair would move by $-0.59$ points between both-correct and unconditioned scoring (assumption-free range $[-1.25, +2.15]$) against a both-correct asymmetry of $-36.80$, and none of their $12$ cross-family CIFAR-10 pairs changes sign. That rescoring substitutes the both-correct rate for the target-correct one, an approximation that errs by $3.54$ points on our own pair, whose source clean errors are larger than any of theirs. The source-target decomposition of Section~\ref{subsec:source_target} gives a partial reconciliation instead. On CIFAR-100 their scratch-trained ResNet-56 and ResNet-164 show our direction against all three non-hybrid ViTs, carried by a target term, while their pre-trained BiT-M models show the opposite direction, which the additive fit assigns to the source term; and our own ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny, before adversarial fine-tuning, already favor ViT-crafted examples under every conditioned protocol (Section~\ref{subsec:external}). Demontis et al.~\cite{demontis2019adversarial} trace transferability to the target's vulnerability and the surrogate's complexity; in our adversarially trained matrices it is the source side that decides the direction. Which difference between the regimes reverses the source effect, adversarial against standard training, ImageNet-21k pre-training or model scale, remains open. A shared protocol with released per-sample records would let it be settled directly.

The four protocols are not interchangeable, and each has a defensible use. The unconditioned rate should not be used for architecture comparison at all: its deviation from the target-correct rate is the target's clean error entering through the law of total probability, exactly so where $p$ is close to one, which means that it measures target weakness in a known proportion and not transferability. The target-correct protocol removes that confound but admits samples the source itself cannot solve, and because those marginal subsets are highly vulnerable and asymmetrically sized, it pulls two directions toward each other by an amount that depends on the clean-accuracy gap. The both-correct protocol scores both directions on identical samples and is the only one of the four that supports paired inference, which is why we treat it as the primary estimate. The successful-source protocol answers a different question, how well an attack that already worked carries over, and its larger value reflects that conditioning on source success selects strong perturbations.

Across four protocols and three training runs, CNN-crafted examples transfer better to the ViT than the reverse \emph{on both CIFAR datasets}, and on CIFAR-10 also under MI-FGSM. The decomposition places that direction on the source side: examples crafted on our ViT-Tiny transfer worse to every target, the external WideResNet included, and in the external pool this ViT-Tiny is the weakest robust source while a robust XCiT-S12 is not. On near-matched SVHN models two protocols report a CNN advantage and two a ViT advantage, and the primary both-correct protocol gives $-0.35$ points with equivalence accepted (Section~\ref{subsec:svhn}). The direction is therefore a property of this unmatched pair: there the protocol changes how large the effect looks and whether equivalence can be claimed, and once the clean accuracies are close it can change the sign as well. The failure of a transformer-specific attack~\cite{zhang2023tgr} to rescue the ViT direction is consistent with a source that is weak for reasons the attack does not reach.

The conditional decomposition separates absolute robustness into a clean-accuracy factor and a conditional-susceptibility factor. In our corrected runs both favor the CNN, so the decomposition and the headline agree. An earlier checkpoint pair, trained before the leakage-free protocol, gave the same absolute ordering but a different division between the two factors (Section~\ref{subsec:training}), so a paper reporting only absolute accuracy would have seen nothing change, while a paper reporting the decomposition from a single run could have drawn a mechanistic conclusion that does not replicate.

The three readings that did not hold share a lesson about evidence. Sparsity measures~\cite{hurley2009comparing} do not establish spatial locality: adversarially trained CNN gradients concentrate more mass on fewer components (Hoyer $0.4928$ versus $0.4561$, consistent in every run but not significant at the run level), yet four spatial descriptors show no difference in how those components are arranged. CLS attention entropy is unchanged under attack at $n=1000$ while attention maps are displaced by a small, depth-increasing amount, and attention-shift results obtained with patch attacks~\cite{gu2022vision, fu2022patchfool} should not be carried over to $L_\infty$-bounded perturbations without measurement. TGR~\cite{zhang2023tgr} costs 14.9 points of white-box strength and returns none of it at the target in our regime. Two behavioral differences do survive: the sparsity ordering, which adversarial training creates, together with an alignment difference in absolute cosine that comes with a near-zero signed mean, so that samples share sensitivity axes but not directions, unlike what a universal-perturbation claim~\cite{moosavi2017universal} would need; and the depth profiles, concentrated in the last residual stage of the CNN and spread across depth in the ViT, with a measured ViT profile that depends on token aggregation.

\subsection{Reporting Requirements}
\label{subsec:implications}

% REF: Turetilmis, Basarili-kaynak: siki ve gevsek okuma (4.77); Standart egitimli hedeflerde oncul p (0.8774)
The measurements translate into requirements for cross-architecture transfer and robustness studies. They are ordered by how far the corresponding choice moved a conclusion in this paper, and the first two matter most.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{State the conditioning set of every transfer number}, including sub-choices such as the strict or loose reading of successful-source, which alone moved our spread by $4.77$ points (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}, Table~\ref{tab:protocol_masks}).
\item \emph{Do not present unconditioned transfer rates as architecture comparisons.} Where only raw rates are available, correct them with Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity} if the targets are adversarially trained and $p$ is close to one, and otherwise with Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity} and a measured $p$, which fell to $0.877$ on standard-trained targets here (Sections~\ref{subsubsec:matrix} and~\ref{subsec:external}).
\item For directional claims, prefer a protocol that scores both directions on identical samples, so that paired inference is available (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}).
\item Report the clean accuracy of every model and the clean-accuracy gap of every compared pair, since the size of the protocol effect scales with that gap (Sections~\ref{subsubsec:spread_gap} and~\ref{subsec:external}).
\item Before attributing a direction to the target, separate source and target effects, for instance with a fixed third model (Section~\ref{subsec:source_target}).
\item State the checkpoint-selection rule, which moved both absolute robust accuracy and the protocol spread by about two points on fixed trajectories (Section~\ref{subsec:selection}).
\item Report absolute robustness together with its factorization into clean accuracy and conditional susceptibility, and base mechanism claims on more than one training run: an earlier checkpoint pair of ours kept the absolute ordering but divided it differently between the two factors (Sections~\ref{subsec:training} and~\ref{subsec:robustness}).
\item For layer-wise analyses, state the token aggregation and the tensor, and check attention claims on hundreds of samples, not on a figure batch (Section~\ref{subsec:attention}).
\end{enumerate}

For practitioners choosing between the two families under the matched mixed-loss recipe on this benchmark, the CNN gives higher absolute robust accuracy and the advantage holds under every view we measured. The WideResNet reference at 62.76\% shows that a larger model trained on extra data with a stronger recipe~\cite{gowal2020uncovering} moves robustness further than the choice between these two families does.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:limitations}

Every architectural conclusion here rests on one adversarially trained pair of small models, ResNet-18 and a ViT-Tiny trained from scratch on $32\times32$ inputs upsampled to $224\times224$, under one mixed-loss PGD adversarial fine-tuning recipe; ViT adversarial training is known to be recipe-sensitive on small datasets~\cite{mo2022adversarial, debenedetti2023light}, a ViT trained with the diffusion-generated data recipe of Wang et al.~\cite{wang2023better} has been reported to surpass WideResNets on CIFAR-10~\cite{wu2025vision}, and the two models differ in parameter count (11.2M versus 5.7M). The 15-point CIFAR-10 magnitude is a property of this unmatched pair and should shrink for pairs matched in clean accuracy, as SVHN and the standard-trained pairs indicate; SVHN itself rests on two pairs, 50 epochs and no AutoAttack, and two runs cannot bound its run-to-run variation. The exact form of Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity} is validated only where $p$ is close to one, which in our data means adversarially trained targets. The mechanism account is incomplete: the composition of marginal subsets covers three of the four protocols, and the successful-source protocol adds a second driver tied to the source's own robustness, whose effect on the spread we could not estimate reliably. The binding pre-registered fit includes SVHN while the pool first used did not, and the CIFAR-100 registration was committed after training had begun. Every model is trained under $\Linf$ at $\eps = 8/255$, so the $L_2$ result probes the protocol and not $L_2$ robustness. We cannot isolate why our two checkpoint sets differ, because the corrected protocol changed the validation handling and the training run at once; the leakage ablation we ran on six full-budget trajectories did not detect an effect of leakage on checkpoint selection, but it answers a weaker question than the one posed, since the treatment used a single validation split and split identity and leakage are not separable in that design. Our TGR result, finally, comes from an adaptation of the original method and not a reproduction of its published setting, and the CIFAR-native ViT control calibrates the upsampling confound without providing a second matched pair.

\subsection{Future Directions}
\label{subsec:future}

The most direct extension is a calibration curve: architecture pairs with controlled clean-accuracy gaps, trained under one shared recipe, would turn the matched-pair prediction from a handful of points into a measured function and show whether the successful-source term depends on source robustness as the external pool suggests. A capacity-matched pair (ViT-Small against ResNet-50, say) would test whether the source effect, the sparsity ordering and the depth profiles belong to the families or to these particular small models. Re-scoring published transfer studies needs per-direction clean accuracies and, for an exact correction, per-sample records; studies that release them make the correction of Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity} a routine step.
