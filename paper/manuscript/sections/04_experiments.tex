The CNN holds the absolute advantage under every attack (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). Under AutoAttack ResNet-18 AT keeps $37.93\pm0.14\%$ of the test set against $29.14\pm0.40\%$ for ViT-Tiny AT, and McNemar's test on the full test set, paired by image, favors the CNN in each of the three seed pairs ($p \leq 1.2\times10^{-84}$ under AutoAttack, $p \leq 1.9\times10^{-120}$ under PGD-10). Clean accuracy accounts for part of the gap. The rest is conditional: of the images each model classifies correctly, PGD-10 flips $48.58\pm0.80\%$ for the CNN and $55.53\pm0.50\%$ for the ViT, and the factorization is exact, $85.78\times0.442 = 37.93$ and $73.53\times0.396 = 29.14$ under AutoAttack. On the $7{,}061\pm68$ images that both models classify correctly the CNN retains $59.86\pm0.35\%$ under PGD-10 and the ViT $46.11\pm0.59\%$ ($51.89$ and $41.15\%$ under AutoAttack).

% FOY:TabloI:19
The conditional PGD-10 column is the vulnerability $w$ of the decomposition: as a target, the ViT is the more vulnerable model. The perturbation-budget sweep and the gradient, attention and feature-drift measurements of the pair are in Appendix~\ref{app:behavior}.

\subsection{From an Additive Split to a Decomposition}
\label{subsec:pair_decomposition}

% FOY:Kaynak-hedef:390
% FOY:TabloIII:54
Under the target-correct protocol the pair's asymmetry, CNN$\rightarrow$ViT minus ViT$\rightarrow$CNN, is positive (Table~\ref{tab:pair_decomposition}), and an additive source-plus-target fit to the three-model matrices that include the WideResNet reference assigns $+5.13$ points of it to the source and $-2.03$ to the target. Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} shows why that split cannot be read as a source effect: with a single ViT among CNNs, the ViT's source term and its similarity to the CNN family enter the same contrast, and the additive fit loads family similarity onto both. Separating them requires a fixed external panel and the multiplicative decomposition used from here on.

% FOY:M9.9:1703,1704,1705,1706,1707,1708,1709,1714,1715,1718,1720,1722
% FOY:TabloII:28,29
The pair itself already separates two of the three terms (Table~\ref{tab:pair_decomposition}). Its log asymmetry, $\log(T_{RV}/T_{VR}) = +0.251$, is the sum of a potential component $\log(w_V/w_R) = +0.136$, which comes from the ViT being the more vulnerable target ($w$ of $55.53$ against $48.58\%$), and a directional residual $r_{RV} = +0.115$ with a run-to-run standard deviation of $0.010$. The potential share is $0.541$, so target vulnerability carries about half of the pair's direction on CIFAR-10. It carries less on CIFAR-100, where the share is $0.283$ and $r_{RV}$ rises to $+0.154$. The symmetric term, $\log\simt_{RV} = -1.083$, enters both directions equally and cannot appear in a two-model asymmetry at all. For one seed pair an image bootstrap gives a standard deviation of $0.030$ for $r_{RV}$, three times its run-to-run spread, a component that the run-level intervals below do not include.

% FOY:M9.9:1726,1727,1728,1729,1739
% FOY:M9.9:1745,1746,1747,1748,1749,1750,1751
A two-model asymmetry cannot say why the ViT looks like a weak source against CNNs in general, because that question concerns the ViT's relation to models outside the pair. Before the registration was frozen, a desk analysis of the eight-model CIFAR-10 pool (Table~\ref{tab:external_pool}) took its four robust CNNs as a panel. Against that panel the ViT's source gap relative to ResNet-18 was $0.422$ log units, of which $0.392$ was symmetric and $0.030$ directional, a symmetric share of $0.928$; these values shaped the predictions and are not tests. At the model level, panel similarity $\sigma$ puts ViT-Tiny far from every other model ($-1.086$ against $-0.694$ to $-0.519$ for the robust CNNs), while $\rho$ stays between $-0.096$ and $+0.083$ for all seven models. The XCiT-S12, which mixes attention with convolutional components, sits among the CNNs on $\sigma$ ($-0.609$), so the one other attention-based model in the pool does not share the low similarity.

\subsection{Panel Tests on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100}
\label{subsec:panel}

% FOY:M9.5:1587,1593
% FOY:M12.2:2290 (turetilmis 2q-1, t %90; on kayit disi)
The registered panel family on CIFAR-10 has three tests, all decided after Holm correction (Table~\ref{tab:panel_tests}). The first, K-a, asks what share of the ViT's source gap against the seven-member CNN panel is symmetric. In the three seed pairs the gap $G_s$ is $0.391$, $0.387$ and $0.390$ log units; its symmetric part is $0.368$, $0.360$ and $0.367$ and its directional part $0.023$, $0.028$ and $0.023$. The mean symmetric share is $q = 0.936$ with a 90\% interval of $[0.925, 0.948]$, every seed clears the predicted $0.75$, and K-a is supported (CIFAR-10 panel of Figure~\ref{fig:source_gap_split}). In terms of the derived quantity $2q - 1$, the symmetric part exceeds the directional part by $0.873$ of the gap ($[0.849, 0.896]$).

% FOY:M9.5:1588
The second test, K-b, asks whether the ViT is a directionally weak source once similarity and vulnerability are removed. Its directional term is $\rho(\cellname{V-scr}) = +0.012$ (seeds $+0.025$, $+0.007$ and $+0.004$), with a 90\% interval of $[-0.007, +0.031]$ well inside the $\pm0.10$ equivalence band, and equivalence is supported (TOST, Holm $p = 2.73\times10^{-3}$). On this panel, on CIFAR-10 and on the log scale, then, ViT-Tiny's weak-source appearance against CNNs is carried by its dissimilarity to them, which holds in both directions.

% FOY:M9.5:1589,1595
The third test, K-c, asks whether that dissimilarity belongs to the family or to individual seeds. The three ViT seeds are much more similar to one another than to the panel ($\Dseed = +0.566$, bootstrap 90\% interval $[0.537, 0.604]$), and the test is supported. The mean pairwise $\log\simt$ between ViT seeds is $-0.443$, against $-0.489$ between the three ResNet-18 seeds, so the ViT runs form a block as tight as the ResNet runs, away from the CNNs (Figure~\ref{fig:similarity_matrix}). A general weakness of the ViT as a source would have lowered this within-family similarity as well.

% FOY:M10.2:1838,1839,1840,1844,1848,1850 ; M9.5:1593 (CIFAR-10 yonlu paylar 0.023-0.028)
% FOY:TabloIII:185 (SVHN hedef-dogru asimetri)
The same family was registered as a binding replication on CIFAR-100, with a seven-member CIFAR-100 panel. K-a and K-c are supported again ($q = 0.840$, $[0.795, 0.885]$; $\Dseed = +0.519$, $[0.469, 0.599]$), and the within-family values repeat ($-0.487$ between ViT seeds, $-0.501$ between ResNet seeds). K-b does not replicate. The directional term is $+0.143$ ($[+0.101, +0.184]$), the TOST $p$ is $0.953$, the 95\% interval excludes zero and the estimate exceeds $0.10$, so equivalence is refuted in the positive direction, and on CIFAR-100 the ViT is also a directionally weaker source. The directional parts of the source gap grow accordingly, to $0.059$, $0.064$ and $0.085$ from $0.023$ to $0.028$ on CIFAR-10, which is why $q$ falls while the symmetric parts stay of the same size. The directional term depends on the dataset: it is large on CIFAR-100, and on SVHN, where no panel was run, the pair's target-correct asymmetry itself changes sign at closely matched clean accuracy (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}).

% FOY:M10.3:1873,1874,1875,1876,1877,1878,1879,1884
On CIFAR-100, ViT-Tiny reaches only $41.94$ to $44.06\%$ clean accuracy, against $62.68$ to $64.65\%$ for ResNet-18 and $53.82$ to $72.57\%$ for the panel, so its $w$ and its target-correct denominators rest on a much smaller and easier subset of images, and part of its directional term may reflect clean accuracy. The both-correct version of these tests, which would hold that subset fixed, has not been run.

% FOY:M11.1:2088,2090,2094,2096 (t ve hiyerarsik bootstrap araliklari, bootstrap p) ; 2105-2110 (kararlar)
% FOY:M11.1:2082,2084 (B 2000) ; 2112,2114 (p tabani 1/(B+1), degisen sinama yok) ; 2078 (etiket: sonradan uretildi)
A hierarchical bootstrap that resamples runs within cells and images shared by all models ($B = 2{,}000$) gives $[0.894, 0.981]$ for $q$ and $[-0.021, +0.046]$ for $\rho(\cellname{V-scr})$ on CIFAR-10, wider than the $t$ intervals, and $[0.800, 0.880]$ and $[+0.106, +0.178]$ on CIFAR-100, slightly narrower. With its intervals and $p$ values in the frozen decision rules no decision changes on either dataset. The two intervals err in opposite directions: the $t$ intervals omit image sampling, and a bootstrap over three runs per cell underestimates the run component. Bootstrap $p$ values are bounded below by $1/(B+1)$ and reach that floor in every test except K-b on CIFAR-100.

\subsection{Architecture by Initialization}
\label{subsec:twobytwo}

% FOY:M9.1:1452,1453,1454,1455,1456,1457,1470,1472,1485
The two-by-two design of Figure~\ref{fig:design_recipe} asks which part of the dissimilarity is architecture and which is initialization, with the input pipeline and the adversarial training recipe held equal across cells. All six new runs passed the registered manipulation checks. Each ImageNet-initialized run exceeded the scratch reference at the first clean epoch by $48.40$ to $69.38$ points, none triggered the collapse rule, and every run stopped early, well before the 80-epoch cap (Section~\ref{subsec:computational}).

% FOY:M9.2:1496,1497,1498,1499,1505,1506,1507,1508,1509,1510,1511,1512,1513
Across the runs of Table~\ref{tab:cells}, only the scratch-trained ViT stands apart on clean accuracy ($73.01$ to $74.10\%$ against $83.39$ to $86.35\%$ in the other cells) and on vulnerability ($w$ of $55.12$ to $55.91\%$ against $47.73$ to $51.11\%$). On panel similarity the two ResNet cells are nearly identical, and ImageNet initialization moves only the ViT (Figure~\ref{fig:cells_interaction}).

% FOY:M9.3:1526,1527,1528,1532,1534,1536
% FOY:M9.4:1545,1549
Each test of the registered two-by-two family is supported (Table~\ref{tab:fb4}). With a matched input pipeline and training from scratch, the ViT is less similar to the CNN panel than the ResNet ($\Asig{scr} = -0.364$, 90\% interval $[-0.415, -0.314]$, Holm $p = 4.09\times10^{-5}$), beyond the predicted size of $-0.20$ (H1). ImageNet initialization narrows that difference more for the ViT than for the ResNet ($\Isig = +0.260$, $[+0.186, +0.335]$, Holm $p = 9.0\times10^{-4}$), and the interaction stays positive on the logit scale ($+0.354$), as the support rule requires (H2). The architecture main effect on the directional term is equivalent to zero ($\Arho = +0.003$, $[-0.020, +0.026]$; TOST Holm $p = 3.79\times10^{-4}$; H3). This combination is the registered outcome region O1 defined in Section~\ref{subsec:statistics}. The masking rule flags no model (Section~\ref{subsec:attack_side}), so the region stands after it, and the tighter margin $\delta = 0.06$ gives the same region.

% FOY:M9.4:1550,1553,1554,1555
% FOY:M12.3:2335,2336,2337,2351,2352,2353,2362-2368 (A10: ResNet-18 turevleri cikarilmis 3 uyeli panel; kesifsel, on kayit disi)
The H1 estimate holds under every registered sensitivity. Panel jackknife moves it between $-0.376$ and $-0.346$, and the hierarchical bootstrap interval is $[-0.393, -0.335]$. Welch intervals, which drop the pooling across cells, are much wider because the new cells have two runs each ($[-0.569, -0.160]$ at $1.10$ degrees of freedom), yet they still exclude zero for H1 and H2. In an exploratory three-member panel without the four ResNet-18 variants, all six CIFAR-10 tests keep their direction and decision and the outcome region stays O1, with similar magnitudes (Appendix~\ref{app:exploratory}).

% FOY:M9.2:1497 ; M9.4:1551,1552,1563
% FOY:M12.1:2234,2235,2238,2251,2252,2255 (A2: rho basit etkileri ve etkilesim, log ve logit; kesifsel)
% YON: [-0.116; -0.058] FOY:M9.4:1551
H3 needs a narrower reading than its decision suggests. It is an equivalence of the architecture main effect, averaged over the two initialization rows, and does not imply a small directional term in every cell. The ImageNet-initialized ViT has a cell mean of $\rho = -0.118$, outside the band, and initialization changes the ViT's directional term by $-0.130$ ($[-0.161, -0.099]$; secondary analysis S2, as predicted). Behind the main effect, the architecture simple effects on $\rho$ are $+0.070$ from scratch and $-0.064$ with ImageNet initialization; their difference, $-0.134$, is negative on the log and the logit scale, and the two simple effects differ in sign only on the log scale (Table~\ref{tab:simple_effects}). On the logit scale H3 is $-0.087$: its 95\% interval excludes zero while the estimate stays inside $\pm0.10$, so the registered rule would return neither support nor refutation on that scale. On the ratio scale the same contrast is $-4.13$ points.

% FOY:M9.4:1561,1562,1564,1565,1566,1568,1569,1575,1576
% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 1 (sahte 2x2 sinamasinda C1 ViT ile C1 ResNet sigma farki

The 32-pixel ResNet-18 of the pair and the 224-pixel ResNet-18 of the design relate to the panel in the same way: $\sigma(\cellname{R224-scr}) - \sigma(\cellname{R32}) = -0.0003$ ($[-0.035, +0.034]$) and the $\rho$ difference is $-0.021$ ($[-0.064, +0.023]$), which is consistent with an unchanged bridge under pooled variances, though not under a Welch 90\% interval ($[-0.120, +0.119]$ for $\sigma$; S1). This bridge, not the $\sigma$ gap seen before registration (Section~\ref{subsec:prereg}), is what H1 tests. Initialization changes the ViT's log vulnerability by $-0.114$ ($[-0.151, -0.077]$) and the ResNet's by $-0.018$ (S3). Against the XCiT-S12 as a secondary panel both $\sigma$ contrasts keep their signs ($-0.284$ and $+0.254$; S6). In the $20$-model residual matrix a Hodge decomposition places $0.919$ of the directional structure in model-level potentials, against $0.100$ at random (S7). The seed-matched pairs (S4, Table~\ref{tab:pair_decomposition}) repeat the pair's potential share in the scratch row ($0.504$ and $0.534$); in the ImageNet row the log ratio is close to zero and the share is undefined.

% FOY:M9.2:1496,1497,1499,1505,1506,1507,1508,1509
% FOY:M12.1:2229,2246 (A2: sigma(V-IN) - sigma(R224-IN), birlesik varyansli t %90 ve Welch %90; kesifsel)
The design cannot separate one alternative explanation of H1. The scratch-trained ViT has both the lowest clean accuracy and the highest vulnerability, and the architecture contrast changes together with them by construction, so part of $\Asig{scr}$ may reflect a less converged solution. The ImageNet-initialized ViT comes close to the clean accuracy of the ResNet cells ($83.39$ and $83.47\%$), yet its panel similarity stays below that of \cellname{R224-IN} ($-0.739$ against $-0.635$). Derived after the results were seen, this contrast, $\sigma(\cellname{V-IN}) - \sigma(\cellname{R224-IN}) = -0.104$, has a pooled-variance 90\% interval of $[-0.159, -0.049]$, while its Welch interval with two runs per cell, $[-0.318, +0.109]$, includes zero (Appendix~\ref{app:exploratory}), so it points to a dissimilarity beyond accuracy only under pooled variances.

\subsection{Attack Side and Gradient Masking}
\label{subsec:attack_side}

% FOY:M9.6:1620,1621,1622,1623,1624,1625,1626,1627,1628,1629,1635,1636,1637,1638,1639,1640
A source gap measured with one attack could reflect how well PGD-10 suits each source. For every source, cross-fitted split-half selection therefore picks the attack that transfers best to the panel among PGD-10, MI-PGD-10 and, for ViT sources, TGR (Section~\ref{subsec:attack_methods}), and the gap is recomputed under each source's chosen attack (Table~\ref{tab:attack_side}). Under PGD-10 the gap between the 32-pixel ResNet-18 and the scratch-trained ViT is $\Gk{PGD} = 0.389$ log units; under the chosen attacks it is $\Gk{best} = 0.374$. The retained fraction is $0.960$ (90\% interval $[0.941, 0.976]$), far above the registered threshold of $0.5$, and the test is supported. Momentum PGD was chosen for all three ViT sources and one of the three ResNet sources, and it closes $0.0155$ log units of the gap.

% FOY:M9.6:1648,1654,1655
TGR was never selected, for any of the five ViT sources. It transfers worse than PGD-10 from every one of them, by $-0.218$ to $-0.147$ log units for the scratch-trained ViTs and by $-0.217$ to $-0.168$ for the ImageNet-initialized ones (Figure~\ref{fig:attack_side}). Among the descriptive design pairs the scratch row retains $0.959$ of its gap, while the ImageNet-row gap of $0.072$ is below the $0.10$ at which the fraction is defined. An earlier matched-budget TGR comparison on the pair is in Appendix~\ref{app:behavior}.

% FOY:M9.7:1666,1667,1668,1669,1670,1671,1673,1675 ; M9.2:1517 ; TabloI:17
AutoAttack was run on the first $1{,}000$ test images of the six new models to check for gradient masking (Table~\ref{tab:cells}). PGD-10 exceeds AutoAttack accuracy by $3.20$ to $7.40$ points, below the registered $10$-point flag, so no model is flagged and the two-by-two decisions are computed on all models as registered.

\subsection{A Descriptive ImageNetV2 Panel}
\label{subsec:imagenet}

% FOY:M10.1:1814,1816,1818,1820,1827 (en buyuk fark matristeki 14 modelde 0.95, Mo ViT-B; 1.20 yalniz kullanilmayan yedek Wong2020Fast)
% FOY:M10.4:1903
The two-by-two design is confined to CIFAR scale and small models. A registered but descriptive panel on ImageNetV2~\cite{recht2019imagenet} asks whether the initialized row has a counterpart among independently trained ImageNet models; it has no decision family and cannot confirm or refute the CIFAR tests. The fourteen models of Section~\ref{subsec:panels} were attacked with PGD-10 through one common preprocessing pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:imagenet}). Every candidate passed the registered preprocessing check, with a largest difference of $0.95$ points between the common pipeline and a model's own among the fourteen models in the matrix ($1.20$ for an unused reserve), and the same image list, identified by its hash, entered the transfer matrix and the gradient run.

% FOY:M10.4:1930,1931,1938,1941
The initialized row is represented by two pairs from Singh et al.~\cite{singh2023revisiting}, a ConvNeXt and a ViT with convolutional stems trained with the same recipe from clean ImageNet checkpoints. In both pairs the transformer is less similar to the CNN panel ($\Ak{\sigma}{1} = -0.167$, image-bootstrap 90\% interval $[-0.234, -0.096]$; $\Ak{\sigma}{2} = -0.118$, $[-0.202, -0.038]$). Their mean, $\AbarIN = -0.142$ ($[-0.197, -0.090]$), meets the registered expectation of at most $-0.10$ with both pairs negative and carries the label ``consistent with expectation''. The result is the descriptive counterpart of the initialized row at natural scale and does not test the two-by-two design; with two pairs, its intervals cover image sampling only.

% FOY:M10.4:1909,1910,1912,1913,1930,1931
Unlike the CIFAR-100 pair, the Singh pairs are matched in clean accuracy and vulnerability. Within each pair clean accuracy and $w$ differ by less than two points (Table~\ref{tab:imagenet}) and the vulnerability contrast is $+0.001$ and $+0.050$ log units, so the lower similarity of these transformers cannot be read off accuracy or vulnerability.

% FOY:M10.4:1932,1939

Its contrast is $\Asig{scr,Liu} = -0.055$ ($[-0.137, +0.022]$), labeled ``uncertain'': at ImageNet scale the scratch-row estimate rests on one pair and is not determined, and the large scratch-row dissimilarity found on CIFAR should not be carried to this scale.

% FOY:M10.4:1909,1910,1930,1931,1932,1943,1945,1947

\subsection{Input-Gradient Alignment}
\label{subsec:gradcos}

% FOY:M10.5:1970,1971,1972,1973,1974,1975,1977,1983,1984,1985,1986,1987,1988,1990
The symmetric term is defined through transfer itself. A registered family therefore asked whether it tracks a quantity measured without any attack: the input-gradient cosine $c_{ij}$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:cij}, averaged over clean test images. In every dataset the rank correlation between $c_{ij}$ and $\log\simt_{ij}$ over all model pairs is high, both raw (H10) and after additive model main effects are removed from both matrices (H10k; Table~\ref{tab:mantel}, Figure~\ref{fig:gradient_alignment}): $\rS = 0.943$ and $0.944$ on CIFAR-10 ($20$ models), $0.981$ and $0.912$ on CIFAR-100 ($15$), and $0.694$ and $0.893$ on ImageNetV2 ($14$). Every one-sided Mantel $p$ sits at the permutation floor of $10^{-4}$, and each test exceeds its predicted size and is supported after Holm correction.

% FOY:M10.5:1996,1997,1998,1999,2000,2001,2009,2010,2011
Two exploratory checks, outside the family, point the same way. Partial Mantel tests that control for both models' log vulnerability give $0.936$, $0.954$ and $0.532$, and adding gradient norm gives $0.937$, $0.941$ and $0.597$. By pair type (lower block of Table~\ref{tab:mantel}), alignment is highest between CNNs and lowest in mixed pairs on both CIFAR datasets. On ImageNetV2, descriptively, the transformer pairs are the more aligned ones ($0.318$ against $0.187$ between CNNs).

% FOY:M11.2:2131,2132,2133 (S10-3 r ve p) ; 2121 (etiket)
% FOY:M11.3:2182,2183,2184 (aile ayiklamali, sira olcegi) ; 2190,2191,2192 (yalniz CNN-CNN, tam matris artigi; ImageNetV2 0.842)
% FOY:M11.3:2171-2176 (aile ici kisitli permutasyon: alti sinamada p 1e-4, sifir ortalamalari -0.008 ile +0.461)
% FOY:M11.3:2200,2202 (R18-R50: c 0.0034, en kucuk, 91 cift; log tau -0.936, sira 55/91)
% KAYNAK: raporlar/konsey_2026-10-29/13_b10_r18_r50_denetimi.md Sec. 6 (GPU denetimi; sayisiz).
Alignment measured at one clean point is associated with similar transfer but is not evidence of a mechanism: it is not measured along the attack path, and the residual statistic removes additive model effects only, leaving the block structure of the two families in place. Since mixed pairs are the least aligned on both CIFAR datasets, part of the association could reflect the split between CNNs and transformers. Exploratory analyses that remove or respect this split leave the association high. With pair-type indicators added to the additive fit (S10-3), $\rS$ is $0.917$, $0.882$ and $0.878$ on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and ImageNetV2; the same adjustment on the rank scale gives $0.905$, $0.892$ and $0.888$, and residuals restricted to CNN pairs give $0.963$, $0.929$ and $0.842$. Permuting models only within their family, so that the null distribution keeps the blocks, leaves all six registered statistics at the permutation floor, with null means between $-0.008$ and $+0.461$. Seeds of one architecture are also close to replicates, which strains the exchangeability that the permutation test assumes. The ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 of Salman et al.~\cite{salman2020adversarially} have the smallest cosine of the $91$ ImageNetV2 pairs ($c = 0.0034$), yet their $\log\simt = -0.936$ ranks $55$th from the bottom; a recomputation on the GPU confirmed the value, with gradients aligned at low spatial frequencies and opposed in the highest band. For this measure a near-zero cosine does not rule out similar transfer.

\subsection{Dependence on Scale, Panel and Dataset}
\label{subsec:scale}

% FOY:M9.5:1603,1604 ; M10.2:1857,1858 ; M9.4:1551,1552
The symmetric findings do not depend on the scale on which rates are compared (Figure~\ref{fig:estimates_forest}). The symmetric share of the ViT's source gap is $1.101$ on the logit scale and $1.542$ on the ratio scale on CIFAR-10, and $1.026$ and $1.216$ on CIFAR-100; a share above one means that on that scale the directional part narrows the gap. H1 and H2 keep their signs on both scales (Table~\ref{tab:fb4}).

% FOY:M9.5:1603,1604 ; M10.2:1857,1858 ; M9.4:1551,1552
The directional term does depend on scale. On the logit scale the ViT's $\rho$ is $-0.304$ ($[-0.320, -0.289]$) on CIFAR-10 and $-0.413$ ($[-0.546, -0.280]$) on CIFAR-100, and on the ratio scale $-14.66$ and $-14.61$ points. These values are large and negative on both datasets, which makes the ViT the directionally stronger source on the logit scale; on CIFAR-100 this reverses a positive log estimate. The log scale is primary because the quasi-symmetry form $T_{ij} = w_j\simt_{ij}$ is additive on it and because the registration fixed it as primary before any of these runs.

% FOY:M9.5:1605,1606 ; M10.2:1859,1860 ; M9.5:1601 ; M10.2:1846 ; M9.4:1549
The directional term also depends on the panel. Leaving out any one panel member moves $\rho(\cellname{V-scr})$ only between $+0.001$ and $+0.022$ on CIFAR-10, but against the XCiT-S12 as a single-member secondary panel it is $+0.198$ to $+0.204$, and on CIFAR-100 against the XCiT-S12 and the Hendrycks et al.\ model $+0.129$ to $+0.221$. Against a single transformer reference, without an interval, the ViT is a directionally weak source.

For this ViT-Tiny, then, the symmetric dissimilarity holds on both CIFAR datasets and on every scale and panel tested, whereas the directional term is small only on the log scale, against a CNN panel and on CIFAR-10.

\subsection{The Conditioning-Protocol Layer}
\label{subsec:transfer}

% FOY:TabloIII:159,160,161,162,163 ; M8.4:1195,1196
Everything above uses one conditioning protocol, target-correct, whose rate $r_{\text{cond}} = T_{ij}$ is the quantity decomposed. Because outcomes are logged per image, the same attack runs can be scored under every protocol of Figure~\ref{fig:protocol_sets}, and on the pair the choice alone moves the asymmetry from $+4.36\pm0.44$ points under target-correct to $+19.37\pm1.27$ under successful-source, a mean per-pair spread of $15.01\pm0.84$ points (Figures~\ref{fig:protocol_settings} and~\ref{fig:protocol_sets}). The direction is positive under all four protocols in all three seed pairs, and also under MI-FGSM (CNN$\rightarrow$ViT $19.07\pm0.33\%$ against ViT$\rightarrow$CNN $15.42\pm0.52\%$, target-correct). The CIFAR-100 and $L_2$ rows of Figure~\ref{fig:protocol_settings} show the same spread and sign (CIFAR-100: $13.83\pm1.30$ points), and the predictions registered for them are evaluated in Appendix~\ref{app:registrations}.

% FOY:Ozdeslikte kullanilan p:641 ; M8.1:1153,1157,1158,1161,1162
% FOY:Standart egitimli hedeflerde oncul p:868,869,870,871
Most of that range is arithmetic. Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity} writes the unconditioned rate as an identity in the target's clean error, the conditioned rate and the premise $p$, the probability that an attack leaves a clean error wrong. Over $36$ off-diagonal directions on adversarially trained targets (two datasets, three seed pairs) the measured $p$ is at least $0.989$ (mean $0.997$) against its upper bound of $1.000$, and the $p = 1$ form reproduces every unconditioned rate to within $0.41$ points. The premise weakens on standard-trained targets, where $p$ falls to $0.877$ and the $p = 1$ form misses by up to $1.58$ points on the clean ViT-Tiny target, the one with the largest clean error; there the general form, Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity}, with measured $p$ applies.

% FOY:M9.9:1764,1768,1770,1771
Proposition~\ref{prop:hscale} states when a protocol change is harmless for the decomposition: on the scale $h = -\log(1 - T)$, unconditioned and target-correct rates should differ only through a target-side potential. In the pool the test of that statement finds a largest calibration term of $|g| = 0.0044$ (smallest $p = 0.933$). The difference between unconditioned and target-correct source effects is $0.016$ of the target-correct source standard deviation on the $h$ scale, against $0.316$ on the ratio scale and $1.655$ on the logit scale, and the difference in target effects correlates at $1.000$ with the target potential on the $h$ scale. Choosing between these two protocols therefore moves target potentials only, provided rates are compared on the $h$ scale.

% FOY:M8.5:1211,1212
% FOY:Turetilmis:214
The identity does not cover the successful-source and both-correct protocols. Images that the target solves on clean input but the source does not are three to four times more fragile than jointly solved ones ($41.2\pm0.8\%$ and $59.1\pm1.7\%$ fooling against $10.0$ and $18.2\%$), and these marginal subsets differ five-fold in size ($1{,}517\pm102$ against $291\pm16$). Target-correct admits both subsets, whose unequal sizes pull the two directions together; both-correct excludes them, and the directional difference reappears $3.91\pm0.32$ points higher. Successful-source selects on the source's own robustness, which does not follow the clean-accuracy gap, so it contributes a second and partly opposing term.

% FOY:Yayilim-temiz fark:1038,1039,1073,1075 ; RobustBench havuzu:1007
% FOY:M9.4:1567 (S5)
% FOY:RobustBench havuzu:1006,1007 (havuz egimleri; 2026-10-31 tab:external_pool alt blogundan metne tasindi, birlesik.md m17)
% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 5 (S5 bandi +-1.5 puan, tasarim sabiti)
% FOY:Secim kurali B:817
Figure~\ref{fig:spread_clean_gap} places every measured pair on the clean-accuracy gap. The spread over the three protocols that the identity covers rises with the gap (slope $+0.796$, Pearson correlation $r_{\mathrm{P}} = 0.957$ over $60$ pairs) and equals the unconditioned-minus-target-correct difference at $57$ of the $60$ points, where the identity tracks it to within $1.09$ points (mean $0.13$). The four-protocol spread does not follow the gap (slope $+0.092$, $r_{\mathrm{P}} = 0.053$). Within the eight-model pool of Table~\ref{tab:external_pool}, over its $28$ pairs, the three-protocol slope is $+0.872$ (model leave-out bootstrap 95\% interval $[+0.534, +1.274]$, $r_{\mathrm{P}} = 0.944$) and the four-protocol slope $+0.559$ ($[-0.205, +4.582]$, $r_{\mathrm{P}} = 0.350$). Out of sample, the two-by-two pairs deviate from the line by $-0.181$ points on average ($[-0.500, +0.138]$), inside the registered $\pm1.5$-point band (S5). The checkpoint-selection rule moves the four-protocol spread by $-2.16\pm0.93$ points without changing the direction (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

% FOY:TabloIII:159,160,161,162,163 ; 185 ; 199 ; Turetilmis:229,230 (SVHN)
SVHN shows what happens when the pair is matched in clean accuracy. With clean gaps of $1.94$ and $1.74$ points in the two seed pairs, the sign of the asymmetry becomes a property of the protocol: target-correct gives $-1.16$ and $-0.85$ points and successful-source $+2.98$ and $+2.41$, while under both-correct the sign-flip permutation $p$ is $0.105$ and TOST accepts equivalence at margins of $\pm1$ to $\pm3$ points. Two seed pairs cannot bound run-to-run variation, so the equivalence rests on the within-run comparison and on the agreement of the two runs. Reported under one protocol only, this pair could crown either architecture as the stronger source.
