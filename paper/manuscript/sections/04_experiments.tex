\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

Unless a protocol is named, every transfer rate below is the target-correct fooling rate $T_{ij}$ of Section~\ref{subsec:metrics}, factored as in Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} into the target's white-box vulnerability $w_j$, the pairwise symmetric similarity $\log\simt_{ij}$ and the directional residual $r_{ij}$. Split-half estimates on the natural-log scale are primary, the logit and ratio scales are sensitivities, and every registered test is listed with its decision rule in Table~\ref{tab:registry}. Cell names follow Table~\ref{tab:design}.

The CNN holds the absolute advantage under every attack (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). Under AutoAttack ResNet-18 AT keeps $37.93\pm0.14\%$ of the test set against $29.14\pm0.40\%$ for ViT-Tiny AT, and McNemar's test on the full test set, paired by image, favors the CNN in each of the three seed pairs ($p < 2\times10^{-84}$ under AutoAttack, $p < 2\times10^{-120}$ under PGD-10). Clean accuracy accounts for part of the gap ($85.78$ against $73.53\%$). The rest is conditional: of the images each model classifies correctly, PGD-10 flips $48.58\pm0.80\%$ for the CNN and $55.53\pm0.50\%$ for the ViT, and the factorization is exact, $85.78\times0.442 = 37.93$ and $73.53\times0.396 = 29.14$ under AutoAttack. On the $7{,}061\pm68$ images that both models classify correctly the CNN retains $59.86\pm0.35\%$ under PGD-10 and the ViT $46.11\pm0.59\%$ ($51.89$ and $41.15\%$ under AutoAttack).

% FOY:TabloI:19
The conditional PGD-10 column is the vulnerability $w$ of the decomposition: as a target, the ViT is the more vulnerable model. An earlier checkpoint pair, trained before the fixed-split protocol (Section~\ref{subsec:training}), gave the same absolute ordering with a different division between clean accuracy and conditional susceptibility, which is why that division is reported only for the three-run pair. The WideResNet-28-10 reference ($62.76\%$ under AutoAttack) stays outside the matched comparison. The perturbation-budget sweep and the gradient, attention and feature-drift measurements of the pair are in Appendix~\ref{app:behavior}.

\subsection{From an Additive Split to a Decomposition}
\label{subsec:pair_decomposition}

% FOY:Kaynak-hedef:390
% FOY:TabloIII:54
Under the target-correct protocol the pair's asymmetry, CNN$\rightarrow$ViT minus ViT$\rightarrow$CNN, is positive (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_settings}), and an additive source-plus-target fit to the three-model matrices that include the WideResNet reference assigns $+5.13$ points of it to the source and $-2.03$ to the target. Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} shows why that split cannot be read as a source effect: with a single ViT among CNNs, the ViT's source term and its similarity to the CNN family enter the same contrast, and the additive fit loads family similarity onto both. Separating them requires a fixed external panel and the multiplicative decomposition used from here on.

% FOY:M9.9:1703,1704,1705,1706,1707,1708,1709,1714,1715,1718,1720,1722
% FOY:TabloII:28,29
The pair itself already separates two of the three terms (Table~\ref{tab:pair_decomposition}). Its log asymmetry, $\log(T_{RV}/T_{VR}) = +0.251$, is the sum of a potential component $\log(w_V/w_R) = +0.136$, which comes from the ViT being the more vulnerable target ($w$ of $55.53$ against $48.58\%$), and a directional residual $r_{RV} = +0.115$ with a run-to-run standard deviation of $0.010$. The potential share is $0.541$, so target vulnerability carries about half of the pair's direction on CIFAR-10. It carries less on CIFAR-100, where the share is $0.283$ and $r_{RV}$ rises to $+0.154$, and almost the same at the last checkpoint ($0.531$). The symmetric term, $\log\simt_{RV} = -1.083$, enters both directions equally and cannot appear in a two-model asymmetry at all. For one seed pair an image bootstrap gives a standard deviation of $0.030$ for $r_{RV}$, three times its run-to-run spread, so the three runs agree to within image noise.

% FOY:M9.9:1726,1727,1728,1729,1739
% FOY:M9.9:1745,1746,1747,1748,1749,1750,1751
A two-model asymmetry cannot say why the ViT looks like a weak source against CNNs in general, because that question concerns the ViT's relation to models outside the pair. Before the registration was frozen, a desk analysis of the eight-model CIFAR-10 pool (Table~\ref{tab:external_pool}) took its four robust CNNs as a panel. Against that panel the ViT's source gap relative to ResNet-18 was $0.422$ log units, of which $0.392$ was symmetric and $0.030$ directional, a symmetric share of $0.928$ ($0.919$ to $0.935$ when one panel member is left out). These values shaped the predictions of Section~\ref{subsec:panel} and are not tests. At the model level, panel similarity $\sigma$ puts ViT-Tiny far from every other model ($-1.086$ against $-0.694$ to $-0.519$ for the robust CNNs), while $\rho$ stays between $-0.096$ and $+0.083$ for all seven models. The XCiT-S12, which mixes attention with convolutional components, sits among the CNNs on $\sigma$ ($-0.609$), so the one other attention-based model in the pool does not share the low similarity.

\subsection{Panel Tests on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100}
\label{subsec:panel}

% FOY:M9.5:1587,1593
The registered panel family on CIFAR-10 has three tests, all decided after Holm correction (Table~\ref{tab:panel_tests}). The first, K-a, asks what share of the ViT's source gap against the seven-member CNN panel is symmetric. In the three seed pairs the gap $G_s$ is $0.391$, $0.387$ and $0.390$ log units; its symmetric part is $0.368$, $0.360$ and $0.367$ and its directional part $0.023$, $0.028$ and $0.023$. The mean symmetric share is $q = 0.936$ with a 90\% interval of $[0.925, 0.948]$, every seed clears the predicted $0.75$, and K-a is supported (Figure~\ref{fig:source_gap_split}a).

% FOY:M9.5:1588
The second test, K-b, asks whether the ViT is a directionally weak source once similarity and vulnerability are removed. Its directional term is $\rho(\cellname{V-scr}) = +0.012$ (seeds $+0.025$, $+0.007$ and $+0.004$), with a 90\% interval of $[-0.007, +0.031]$ well inside the $\pm0.10$ equivalence band, and equivalence is supported (TOST, Holm $p = 2.73\times10^{-3}$). On this panel and on the log scale, then, ViT-Tiny's weak-source appearance against CNNs is carried by its dissimilarity to them, which holds in both directions.

% FOY:M9.5:1589,1595
The third test, K-c, asks whether that dissimilarity belongs to the family or to individual seeds. The three ViT seeds are much more similar to one another than to the panel ($\Dseed = +0.566$, bootstrap 90\% interval $[0.537, 0.604]$), and the test is supported. The mean pairwise $\log\simt$ between ViT seeds is $-0.443$, against $-0.489$ between the three ResNet-18 seeds, so the ViT runs form a block as tight as the ResNet runs do, and the block lies away from the CNNs (Figure~\ref{fig:similarity_matrix}).

% FOY:M10.2:1838,1839,1840,1844,1848,1850 ; M9.5:1593 (CIFAR-10 yonlu paylar 0.023-0.028)
% FOY:TabloIII:185 (SVHN hedef-dogru asimetri)
The same family was registered as a binding replication on CIFAR-100, with a seven-member CIFAR-100 panel. K-a and K-c are supported again ($q = 0.840$, $[0.795, 0.885]$; $\Dseed = +0.519$, $[0.469, 0.599]$), and the within-family values repeat ($-0.487$ between ViT seeds, $-0.501$ between ResNet seeds). The symmetric share of the ViT's source gap and the greater similarity of ViT seeds to one another than to the panel therefore replicate on CIFAR-100 with three seeds. K-b does not. The directional term is $+0.143$ ($[+0.101, +0.184]$), the TOST $p$ is $0.953$, the 95\% interval excludes zero and the estimate exceeds $0.10$, so equivalence is refuted in the positive direction. The directional parts of the source gap grow accordingly, to $0.059$, $0.064$ and $0.085$ from $0.023$ to $0.028$ on CIFAR-10, which is why $q$ falls while the symmetric parts stay of the same size. The directional term depends on the dataset: it is large on CIFAR-100, and on SVHN, where no panel was run, even the pair's target-correct asymmetry reverses sign (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}). The statement that the directional term is small is therefore restricted to CIFAR-10.

% FOY:M10.3:1873,1874,1875,1876,1877,1878,1879,1884
One feature of CIFAR-100 limits how far the ViT quantities there can be read. ViT-Tiny reaches only $41.94$ to $44.06\%$ clean accuracy, against $62.68$ to $64.65\%$ for ResNet-18 and $53.82$ to $72.57\%$ for the panel, so its $w$ and its target-correct denominators rest on a much smaller and easier subset of images, and part of its directional term may reflect clean accuracy. The both-correct version of these tests, which would hold that subset fixed, has not been run. The sensitivities in the lower block of Table~\ref{tab:panel_tests} are taken up in Section~\ref{subsec:scale}.

The two-by-two design of Table~\ref{tab:design} asks which part of the dissimilarity is architecture and which is initialization, with the input pipeline and the adversarial training recipe held equal across cells. All six new runs passed the registered manipulation checks. Each ImageNet-initialized run exceeded the scratch reference at the first clean epoch by $48.40$ to $69.38$ points, none triggered the collapse rule, and every run stopped early, well before the 80-epoch cap (Section~\ref{subsec:computational}).

% FOY:M9.2:1496,1497,1498,1499,1505,1506,1507,1508,1509,1510,1511,1512,1513
Table~\ref{tab:cells} gives the cells run by run. Only the scratch-trained ViT stands apart on clean accuracy ($73.01$ to $74.10\%$ against $83.39$ to $86.35\%$ in the other cells) and on vulnerability ($w$ of $55.12$ to $55.91\%$ against $47.73$ to $51.11\%$). On panel similarity the two ResNet cells are nearly identical, and ImageNet initialization moves only the ViT (Figure~\ref{fig:cells_interaction}).

% FOY:M9.3:1526,1527,1528,1532,1534,1536
% FOY:M9.4:1545,1549
Each test of the registered two-by-two family is supported (Table~\ref{tab:fb4}). With a matched input pipeline and training from scratch, the ViT is less similar to the CNN panel than the ResNet ($\Asig{scr} = -0.364$, 90\% interval $[-0.415, -0.314]$, Holm $p = 4.09\times10^{-5}$), beyond the predicted size of $-0.20$ (H1). ImageNet initialization narrows that difference more for the ViT than for the ResNet ($\Isig = +0.260$, $[+0.186, +0.335]$, Holm $p = 9.0\times10^{-4}$), and the interaction stays positive on the logit scale ($+0.354$), as the support rule requires (H2). The architecture main effect on the directional term is equivalent to zero ($\Arho = +0.003$, $[-0.020, +0.026]$; TOST Holm $p = 3.79\times10^{-4}$; H3). This combination is the registered outcome region O1 defined in Section~\ref{subsec:statistics}. The masking rule flags no model (Section~\ref{subsec:attack_side}), so the region stands after it, and the tighter margin $\delta = 0.06$ gives the same region.

% FOY:M9.4:1550,1553,1554,1555
The H1 estimate holds under every registered sensitivity. Panel jackknife moves it between $-0.376$ and $-0.346$, and the hierarchical bootstrap interval is $[-0.393, -0.335]$. Welch intervals, which drop the pooling across cells, are much wider because the new cells have two runs each ($[-0.569, -0.160]$ at $1.10$ degrees of freedom), yet they still exclude zero for H1 and H2.

% FOY:M9.2:1497 ; M9.4:1551,1552,1563
H3 needs a narrower reading than its decision suggests. It is an equivalence of the architecture main effect, averaged over the two initialization rows, and does not imply a small directional term in every cell. The ImageNet-initialized ViT has a cell mean of $\rho = -0.118$, outside the band, and initialization changes the ViT's directional term by $-0.130$ ($[-0.161, -0.099]$; secondary analysis S2, as predicted). On the logit scale H3 is $-0.087$: its 95\% interval excludes zero while the estimate stays inside $\pm0.10$, so the registered rule would return neither support nor refutation on that scale. On the ratio scale the same contrast is $-4.13$ points.

The 32-pixel ResNet-18 of the pair and the 224-pixel ResNet-18 of the design relate to the panel in the same way: $\sigma(\cellname{R224-scr}) - \sigma(\cellname{R32}) = -0.0003$ ($[-0.035, +0.034]$) and the $\rho$ difference is $-0.021$ ($[-0.064, +0.023]$), so the change of stem and input pipeline does not move the bridge (S1). Since the $\sigma$ gap between the pair's ViT and the 32-pixel ResNet had been seen before registration (Section~\ref{subsec:prereg}), this bridge is what H1 adds: a ResNet-18 trained on the ViT's 224-pixel pipeline stands where the 32-pixel one stood. \subsection{Attack Side and Gradient Masking}
\label{subsec:attack_side}

% FOY:M9.6:1620,1621,1622,1623,1624,1625,1626,1627,1628,1629,1635,1636,1637,1638,1639,1640
A source gap measured with one attack could reflect how well PGD-10 suits each source. For every source, cross-fitted split-half selection therefore picks the attack that transfers best to the panel among PGD-10, MI-PGD-10 and, for ViT sources, TGR (Section~\ref{subsec:attack_methods}), and the gap is recomputed under each source's chosen attack (Table~\ref{tab:attack_side}). Under PGD-10 the gap between the 32-pixel ResNet-18 and the scratch-trained ViT is $\Gk{PGD} = 0.389$ log units; under the chosen attacks it is $\Gk{best} = 0.374$. The retained fraction is $0.960$ (90\% interval $[0.941, 0.976]$), far above the registered threshold of $0.5$, and the test is supported. Momentum PGD was chosen for all three ViT sources and one of the three ResNet sources, and it closes $0.0155$ log units of the gap.

% FOY:M9.6:1648,1654,1655
TGR was never selected, for any of the five ViT sources. It transfers worse than PGD-10 from every one of them, by $-0.218$ to $-0.147$ log units for the scratch-trained ViTs and by $-0.217$ to $-0.168$ for the ImageNet-initialized ones (Figure~\ref{fig:attack_side}). TGR was run with its original step size and adapted to these inputs, so the result bounds our adaptation of the method. In the descriptive pairs of the design the scratch row retains $0.959$ of its gap. In the ImageNet row the PGD-10 gap is only $0.072$, below the $0.10$ at which the retained fraction is defined, so no fraction is reported. An earlier matched-budget TGR comparison on the pair is in Appendix~\ref{app:behavior}.

AutoAttack was run on the first $1{,}000$ test images of the six new models to check for gradient masking (Table~\ref{tab:cells}). PGD-10 exceeds AutoAttack accuracy by $3.20$ to $7.40$ points, below the registered $10$-point flag, so no model is flagged and the two-by-two decisions are computed on all models as registered.

\subsection{A Descriptive ImageNetV2 Panel}
\label{subsec:imagenet}

% FOY:M10.1:1818,1820,1827
% FOY:M10.4:1903
The two-by-two design is confined to CIFAR scale and small models. A registered but descriptive panel on ImageNetV2~\cite{recht2019imagenet} asks whether the initialized row has a counterpart among independently trained ImageNet models; it has no decision family and cannot confirm or refute the CIFAR tests. Fourteen RobustBench models were attacked with PGD-10 at $\eps = 4/255$ and $\alpha = 1/255$ on $2{,}000$ MatchedFrequency images, two per class, through one common preprocessing pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:imagenet}). Every candidate passed the registered preprocessing check, with a largest difference of $1.20$ points between the common pipeline and a model's own, and the same image list, identified by its hash, entered the transfer matrix and the gradient run.

% FOY:M10.4:1930,1931,1938,1941
The initialized row is represented by two pairs from Singh et al.~\cite{singh2023revisiting}, a ConvNeXt and a ViT with convolutional stems trained with the same recipe from clean ImageNet checkpoints. In both pairs the transformer is less similar to the CNN panel ($A^{\sigma} = -0.167$, image-bootstrap 90\% interval $[-0.234, -0.096]$; and $-0.118$, $[-0.202, -0.038]$). Their mean, $\bar{A}^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{IN}} = -0.142$ ($[-0.197, -0.090]$), meets the registered expectation of at most $-0.10$ with both pairs negative and carries the label ``consistent with expectation''. At ImageNet scale, then, in two CNN-transformer pairs initialized from clean models and trained with the same recipe, the transformers are less similar to the classical CNN panel. This is the descriptive counterpart of the initialized row at natural scale and does not test the two-by-two design; with two pairs, its intervals cover image sampling only.

% FOY:M10.4:1909,1910,1912,1913,1930,1931

Within each pair clean accuracy and $w$ differ by less than two points (Table~\ref{tab:imagenet}) and the vulnerability contrast is $+0.001$ and $+0.050$ log units, so the lower similarity of these transformers cannot be read off accuracy or vulnerability.

\subsection{Input-Gradient Alignment}
\label{subsec:gradcos}

% FOY:M10.5:1970,1971,1972,1973,1974,1975,1977,1983,1984,1985,1986,1987,1988,1990
The symmetric term is defined through transfer itself. A registered family therefore asked whether it is predicted by a quantity measured without any attack: the input-gradient cosine $c_{ij}$ of Equation~\eqref{eq:cij}, averaged over clean test images, which is the gradient similarity of Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2024why}. In every dataset the rank correlation between $c_{ij}$ and $\log\simt_{ij}$ over all model pairs is high, both raw and after additive model main effects are removed from both matrices (Table~\ref{tab:mantel}, Figure~\ref{fig:gradient_alignment}): $\rS = 0.943$ and $0.944$ on CIFAR-10 ($20$ models), $0.981$ and $0.912$ on CIFAR-100 ($15$), and $0.694$ and $0.893$ on ImageNetV2 ($14$). Every one-sided Mantel $p$ sits at the permutation floor of $10^{-4}$, and each test exceeds its predicted size and is supported after Holm correction. The symmetric term is thus predicted by input-gradient alignment, a quantity measured independently of transfer and attack, also after model-level main effects are removed.

% FOY:M10.5:1996,1997,1998,1999,2000,2001,2009,2010,2011
Two exploratory checks, outside the family, point the same way. Partial Mantel tests that control for both models' log vulnerability give $0.936$, $0.954$ and $0.532$, and adding gradient norm gives $0.937$, $0.941$ and $0.597$. By pair type (lower block of Table~\ref{tab:mantel}), alignment is highest between CNNs and lowest in mixed pairs on both CIFAR datasets. On ImageNetV2 the transformer pairs are the more aligned ones ($0.318$ against $0.187$ between CNNs), a descriptive observation that the CIFAR panels do not share.

% FOY:yok (13_b10_r18_r50_denetimi.md; sayi verilmez, brif Sec. 13 sonrasi kapanan is)
% FOY:M10.5:2009 (cift turu sayilari tabloda); aile ayiklamali ve yalniz CNN-CNN
% Spearman foyde yok (brif Sec. 9), sinir sayisiz yazildi.
Alignment measured at one clean point is a necessary condition for similar transfer and not evidence of a mechanism: it is not measured along the attack path, and the residual statistic removes additive model effects only. In particular it leaves the block structure of the two families in place. Most pairs are CNN pairs or mixed pairs, mixed pairs are the least aligned on both CIFAR datasets (Table~\ref{tab:mantel}), and part of the association may therefore reflect the split between CNNs and transformers; seeds of one architecture are also close to replicates, which strains the exchangeability that the permutation test assumes. One near-zero entry of the ImageNetV2 matrix, between the ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 of Salman et al.~\cite{salman2020adversarially}, was recomputed and is genuine: the two gradients align at low spatial frequencies and oppose each other in the highest-frequency band, so the total cancels.

\subsection{Dependence on Scale, Panel and Dataset}
\label{subsec:scale}

% FOY:M9.5:1603,1604 ; M10.2:1857,1858 ; M9.4:1551,1552
The symmetric findings do not depend on the scale on which rates are compared (Figure~\ref{fig:estimates_forest}). The symmetric share of the ViT's source gap is $1.101$ on the logit scale and $1.542$ on the ratio scale on CIFAR-10, and $1.026$ and $1.216$ on CIFAR-100; a share above one means that on that scale the directional part narrows the gap. H1 and H2 keep their signs on both scales (Table~\ref{tab:fb4}).

% FOY:M9.5:1603,1604 ; M10.2:1857,1858 ; M9.4:1551,1552
The directional term does depend on scale. On the logit scale the ViT's $\rho$ is $-0.304$ ($[-0.320, -0.289]$) on CIFAR-10 and $-0.413$ ($[-0.546, -0.280]$) on CIFAR-100, and on the ratio scale $-14.66$ and $-14.61$ points. The sign reverses: on the log scale the ViT is, if anything, the directionally weaker source, and on the logit scale it is the directionally stronger one by a margin far outside the $\pm0.10$ band. H3 reverses in the same way (Section~\ref{subsec:twobytwo}). The log scale is primary because the quasi-symmetry form $T_{ij} = w_j\simt_{ij}$ of Section~\ref{subsec:decomposition_def} is additive on it, and because the registration fixed it as primary, with the logit and ratio scales as sensitivities, before any of these runs.

% FOY:M9.5:1605,1606 ; M10.2:1859,1860 ; M9.5:1601 ; M10.2:1846 ; M9.4:1549
The directional term also depends on the panel. Leaving out any one panel member moves $\rho(\cellname{V-scr})$ only between $+0.001$ and $+0.022$ on CIFAR-10, but against the XCiT-S12 as a single-member secondary panel it is $+0.198$ to $+0.204$, and on CIFAR-100 against the XCiT-S12 and the Hendrycks et al.\ model $+0.129$ to $+0.221$. Against a transformer reference the ViT is a directionally weak source. Every registered decision is unchanged at $\delta = 0.06$ on both CIFAR datasets and in the two-by-two family.

What survives is therefore a narrow claim. A symmetric dissimilarity carries the ViT's weak-source appearance against robust CNNs on both CIFAR datasets and on every scale and panel tested, and the descriptive ImageNetV2 panel points the same way, whereas the directional term is small only on the log scale, against a CNN panel and on CIFAR-10. Section~\ref{subsec:limitations} lists what remains open.

\subsection{The Conditioning-Protocol Layer}
\label{subsec:transfer}

% FOY:TabloIII:159,160,161,162,163 ; M8.4:1195,1196
Everything above uses one conditioning protocol, target-correct, whose rate $r_{\text{cond}} = T_{ij}$ is the quantity decomposed. Because outcomes are logged per image, the same attack runs can be scored under every protocol of Table~\ref{tab:protocol_masks}, and on the pair the choice alone moves the asymmetry from $+4.36\pm0.44$ points under target-correct to $+19.37\pm1.27$ under successful-source, a mean per-pair spread of $15.01\pm0.84$ points (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_settings}, Figure~\ref{fig:protocol_sets}). The direction is positive under all four protocols in all three seed pairs, and also under MI-FGSM (CNN$\rightarrow$ViT $19.07\pm0.33\%$ against ViT$\rightarrow$CNN $15.42\pm0.52\%$, target-correct). The CIFAR-100 and $L_2$ columns of Table~\ref{tab:protocol_settings} show the same spread and sign, and the predictions registered for them are evaluated in Appendix~\ref{app:registrations}.

% FOY:Ozdeslikte kullanilan p:641 ; M8.1:1157,1158,1161,1162
% FOY:Standart egitimli hedeflerde oncul p:868,869,870,871
Most of that range is arithmetic. Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity} writes the unconditioned rate as an identity in the target's clean error, the conditioned rate and the premise $p$, the probability that an attack leaves a clean error wrong. Over $36$ off-diagonal directions on adversarially trained targets (two datasets, three seed pairs) the measured $p$ is at least $0.989$ (mean $0.997$) against its upper bound of $1.000$, and the $p = 1$ form reproduces every unconditioned rate to within $0.41$ points. The premise weakens on standard-trained targets, where $p$ falls to $0.877$ and the $p = 1$ form misses by up to $1.58$ points on the clean ViT-Tiny target, the one with the largest clean error; there the general form, Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity}, with measured $p$ applies.

% FOY:M9.9:1764,1768,1770,1771
Proposition~\ref{prop:hscale} states when a protocol change is harmless for the decomposition: on the scale $h = -\log(1 - T)$, unconditioned and target-correct rates should differ only through a target-side potential. In the pool the test of that statement finds a largest calibration term of $|g| = 0.0044$ (smallest $p = 0.933$). The difference between unconditioned and target-correct source effects is $0.016$ of the target-correct source standard deviation on the $h$ scale, against $0.316$ on the ratio scale and $1.655$ on the logit scale, and the difference in target effects correlates at $1.000$ with the target potential on the $h$ scale. Choosing between these two protocols therefore moves target potentials only, provided rates are compared on the $h$ scale.

% FOY:M8.5:1211,1212
% FOY:Turetilmis:214
The identity does not cover the successful-source and both-correct protocols. Images that the target solves on clean input but the source does not are three to four times more fragile than jointly solved ones ($41.2\pm0.8\%$ and $59.1\pm1.7\%$ fooling against $10.0$ and $18.2\%$), and these marginal subsets differ five-fold in size ($1{,}517\pm102$ against $291\pm16$). Target-correct admits both subsets, whose unequal sizes pull the two directions together; both-correct excludes them, and the directional difference reappears $3.91\pm0.32$ points higher. Successful-source selects on the source's own robustness, which does not follow the clean-accuracy gap, so it contributes a second and partly opposing term.

% FOY:Yayilim-temiz fark:1038,1039,1073,1075 ; RobustBench havuzu:1007
% FOY:M9.4:1567 (S5)
% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 5 (S5 bandi +-1.5 puan, tasarim sabiti)
% FOY:Secim kurali B:817
Figure~\ref{fig:spread_clean_gap} places every measured pair on the clean-accuracy gap. The spread over the three protocols that the identity covers rises with the gap (slope $+0.796$, Pearson $r = 0.957$ over $60$ pairs) and equals the unconditioned-minus-target-correct difference at $57$ of the $60$ points, where the identity tracks it to within $1.09$ points (mean $0.13$). The four-protocol spread does not follow the gap (slope $+0.092$, $r = 0.053$). Within the pool the three-protocol slope is $+0.872$ (Table~\ref{tab:external_pool}). Out of sample, the two-by-two pairs deviate from the line by $-0.181$ points on average ($[-0.500, +0.138]$), inside the registered $\pm1.5$-point band (S5). The checkpoint-selection rule moves the four-protocol spread by $-2.16\pm0.93$ points without changing the direction (Appendix~\ref{app:selection}).

% FOY:TabloIII:159,160,161,162,163 ; 185 ; 199 ; Turetilmis:229,230 (SVHN)
SVHN shows what happens when the pair is matched in clean accuracy. With clean gaps of $1.94$ and $1.74$ points in the two seed pairs, the sign of the asymmetry becomes a property of the protocol: target-correct gives $-1.16$ and $-0.85$ points and successful-source $+2.98$ and $+2.41$, while under both-correct the sign-flip permutation $p$ is $0.105$ and TOST accepts equivalence at margins of $\pm1$ to $\pm3$ points. Two seed pairs cannot bound run-to-run variation, so the equivalence rests on the within-run comparison and on the agreement of the two runs. Reported under one protocol only, this pair could crown either architecture as the stronger source.
