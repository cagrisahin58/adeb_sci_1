\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked how much the conditioning set of a transfer rate decides what a CNN-versus-ViT robustness comparison concludes, and answered it by varying only that set on fixed models and data. The conditioning set decides more than retraining does: on one adversarially trained pair it moves the measured asymmetry across a range several times wider than the across-run range of any single protocol.

Most of that range is predictable. The part between unconditioned and target-correct rates is the target's clean error entering through the law of total probability, an exact correction where its premise has been measured close to one and an upper bound where it has not; the successful-source protocol answers a different question; and the two protocols that remain defensible still differ by a composition term with a closed form. A transfer number is therefore interpretable only together with the set it was computed on.

What the asymmetry says about the architectures is narrower than its size suggests. Its direction comes from the examples crafted on this particular ViT-Tiny, which transfer worse to every target, and not from a weaker ViT target; its magnitude comes from the clean-accuracy gap of the pair and shrinks for pairs matched in clean accuracy, in our own models and in an external pool alike, while the checkpoint-selection rule shifts it by a few points. Absolute robustness favored the CNN under every attack, and three readings that architecture comparisons readily suggest did not survive direct measurement.

Our study covers three datasets and a single adversarially trained model pair, with standard-trained and external models used only for the protocol analysis, so the size of the effect for other pairs remains to be calibrated. Because every protocol estimate is recomputed from per-sample records, the comparison can be repeated on other models without new attacks, and the reporting requirements of Section~\ref{subsec:implications} state what a reader needs to make that possible.
