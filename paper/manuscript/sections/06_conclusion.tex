\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether an adversarially trained ViT that looks like a weak transfer source against CNNs crafts weaker examples or is simply different from the models it attacks. Measured against a fixed panel of robust CNNs, with the vulnerability of each target taken out, most of ViT-Tiny's source gap on CIFAR-10 is symmetric: the ViT and the CNNs transfer poorly to each other in both directions, and the ViT seeds resemble one another more than they resemble the panel. Trained from scratch on a matched input pipeline and recipe, the ViT is less similar to the panel than the ResNet, a contrast that also carries the ViT's lower clean accuracy; ImageNet initialization removes more of the dissimilarity for the ViT than for the ResNet, and on the log scale architecture leaves the directional term unchanged. Momentum PGD and our adaptation of token gradient regularization do not close the gap, and none of the new models shows signs of gradient masking.

The directional term is small only on the log scale, against a CNN panel and on CIFAR-10. It grows on CIFAR-100, and on the logit scale it is large and of opposite sign, so that there the ViT is the directionally stronger source; the evidence from ImageNet models is descriptive. Input-gradient alignment tracks pairwise similarity on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and ImageNetV2; we read it as a necessary condition for similar transfer, which leaves the mechanism open. Our training covers three datasets, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and SVHN, with ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny at CIFAR scale; ImageNetV2 enters only through independently trained checkpoints, so whether the pattern holds for larger models trained from scratch at ImageNet scale remains to be measured.

A cross-architecture transfer comparison becomes interpretable when it states its conditioning set, measures each target's vulnerability separately from the similarity between models, does so against models outside the compared pair, and names the scale on which direction is read; Section~\ref{subsec:implications} turns these into reporting requirements.
