\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented a mechanistic analysis of why CNN and Vision Transformer architectures exhibit different adversarial vulnerability patterns. Moving beyond simple robustness comparisons, we investigated the underlying causes of behavioral differences through transfer attack analysis, gradient characterization, and layer-wise feature degradation studies.

Our analysis reveals that a consistent robustness gap exists between architectures under AutoAttack evaluation, with model capacity playing a substantial role beyond architectural choice alone. The observed transfer asymmetry---CNN-generated adversarial examples transfer to ViTs at 41.2\% compared to 36.1\% in the reverse direction---is partially explained by gradient sparsity: CNN gradients concentrate perturbation energy on localized, generic features that both architectures rely on, while ViT gradients distribute perturbations more uniformly across the input. Contrary to initial expectations, cross-sample gradient alignment is nearly identical between architectures under adversarial training, suggesting that alignment alone does not drive the transfer difference. We also identify a ``semantic drift'' mechanism whereby adversarial perturbations cause progressive directional degradation in ViT feature representations, with cosine similarity decreasing from 0.995 to 0.958 across transformer blocks despite modest norm changes.

This work connects gradient sparsity to cross-architecture transfer attack asymmetry and identifies gradient alignment convergence under adversarial training as a phenomenon warranting further investigation, offering actionable understanding rather than mere performance comparison. Our systematic layer-wise analysis characterizes how adversarial perturbations propagate through ViT layers, and we derive evidence-based recommendations for robust system design. We publicly release our analysis pipeline to enable future work extending these findings to other architectures and datasets.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
% Closing
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Understanding \textit{why} architectures behave differently under adversarial attack---not just \textit{how much}---is essential for developing targeted defenses. Our analysis reveals that the robustness gap between CNNs and ViTs is not arbitrary but stems from differences in gradient structure, particularly sparsity: CNN gradients concentrate sensitivity in localized regions while ViT gradients distribute it more uniformly. The near-identical gradient alignment under adversarial training further suggests that training regime, not architecture alone, shapes certain gradient properties---a finding that opens new questions about the interaction between defense methods and architectural inductive biases. We hope this mechanistic perspective guides future research toward more principled defense design.
