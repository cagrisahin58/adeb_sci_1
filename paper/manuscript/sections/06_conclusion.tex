\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked how much of the disagreement in CNN and Vision Transformer robustness comparisons comes from the measurement and not from the models, and answered it by varying only how the comparison is scored, with everything else held fixed.

The measurement dominates: on identical adversarially trained ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny pairs, the cross-architecture transfer asymmetry ranges from $+4.4$ to $+19.4$ points across four conditioning protocols, whereas retraining both architectures from scratch moves that same quantity by under $1.5$ points. That $15.01$-point spread decomposes into $9.21$ points that an identity we derive removes in closed form and $5.79$ points contributed by a protocol that estimates a different quantity. The $3.91$ points of genuine discretion that survive the correction, between two defensible protocols, are the part a reporting standard has to address. Unconditioned rates are not a valid basis for such a comparison at all: in a $3\times3$ matrix that adds a WideResNet-28-10 reference, their deviation from conditioned rates is algebraically equal to the target's clean error scaled by one minus the conditioned rate, an identity that holds to within $0.41$ points across $36$ measured directions and that applies to any published raw rate whose targets satisfy the measured antecedent. The CIFAR-100 replication and the checkpoint-selection ablation point the same way: measurement choices that papers rarely report are of the same order as the effects being reported. The direction is stable across protocols, seeds, and attack families on both CIFAR datasets, but not across datasets: on SVHN, where the same recipe leaves the two architectures only $1.85$ points apart in clean accuracy, two of the four protocols reverse the sign and the primary protocol accepts equivalence. The direction, in other words, holds while the models are unmatched, and our three-target analysis associates it with the target's own vulnerability ($r = 0.986$).

In the conditional decomposition of absolute robustness both factors favor the CNN in our corrected runs, but the attribution between them differed on an earlier checkpoint set whose absolute ordering was identical.

Three readings did not survive direct measurement: adversarially trained CNN gradients are sparser but not spatially more localized, CLS attention entropy does not change under $L_\infty$ attack at $n=1000$, and a transformer-specific transfer attack yields no transfer gain in this regime. Only the third is a published claim; the first two are interpretations that our own earlier figures encouraged. Two behavioral differences did survive and reproduce across independent checkpoint sets: the gradient sparsity ordering, which adversarial training creates, and the depth profile of adversarial damage, concentrated late in the CNN and spread across depth in the ViT.

From these measurements we distilled a short set of reporting requirements, of which the first two matter most: state the conditioning protocol and prefer paired ones, and do not present unconditioned transfer rates as architecture comparisons. Our study covers three datasets and a single model pair, so the size of the protocol effect for other architecture pairs remains to be calibrated; the mechanism behind it, an unequal clean-accuracy gap between targets, is not specific to our setting. The pipeline, the per-sample logs and the analysis scripts will be released on acceptance so that the protocol comparison can be reproduced and extended.
