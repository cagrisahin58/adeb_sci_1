\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The study centers on one adversarially trained pair, a ResNet-18 and a ViT-Tiny with three seeds each (Section~\ref{subsec:setup}). Each target-correct transfer rate is factored into the target's white-box vulnerability, a similarity shared by both directions and a directional residual, and the last two are averaged over a fixed panel of robust CNNs that contains neither model (Sections~\ref{subsec:decomposition_def} and~\ref{subsec:panels}). A two-by-two design crosses architecture with ImageNet initialization (Section~\ref{subsec:design}). All decision rules were registered before the runs they concern (Sections~\ref{subsec:statistics} and~\ref{subsec:prereg}), and Figure~\ref{fig:study_overview} summarizes the design.

\subsection{Threat Model and Models}
\label{subsec:setup}

% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 2-3 (eps, alpha, girdi hatti, timm adlari); EK Sec.
% 5.2 (ImageNetV2 eps 4/255, alpha 1/255); parametre sayilari eski metin
% 03:43 ve 03:47 (tasarim olgusu, sonuc degil).
All attacks are untargeted and bounded in $\Linf$ norm, $\mathcal{B}_\eps(x) = \{x' : \|x' - x\|_\infty \leq \eps\}$, with $\eps = 8/255$ on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} and $\eps = 4/255$ on ImageNetV2.

The CIFAR ResNet-18~\cite{he2016deep}, called R32 here, has a $3\times3$ stem, 11.2M parameters and $32\times32$ inputs. ViT-Tiny (\texttt{vit\_tiny\_patch16\_224} in \texttt{timm}~\cite{rw2019timm}; 5.7M parameters, $16\times16$ patches, 12 blocks of width 192 with 3 heads) reads inputs upsampled bilinearly from 32 to 224 pixels. R224, the \texttt{timm} \texttt{resnet18} with its $7\times7$ stem, receives the same upsampled input. ImageNet weights come from \texttt{deit\_tiny\_patch16\_224.fb\_in1k}~\cite{touvron2021training} and \texttt{resnet18.a1\_in1k}~\cite{wightman2021resnet}. CIFAR models read raw pixels in $[0,1]$; ImageNet-initialized ones apply ImageNet normalization inside the model. The robust WideResNet-28-10 of Gowal et al.~\cite{gowal2020uncovering}, trained with extra data and a stronger recipe, is an unmatched reference point.

\subsection{Attack Methods}
\label{subsec:attack_methods}

% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 5 B5 (MI-PGD mu 1.0, alpha eps/10; TGR alpha 2/255,
% mu 1.0, gamma 0.5; adim 10); eski metin 03:62-69 ve 03:171 (TGR uyarlamasi).
PGD~\cite{madry2018towards} takes ten signed-gradient steps on the cross-entropy, of size $\alpha = 2/255$ ($1/255$ on ImageNetV2), from a uniform random start, projecting onto $\mathcal{B}_\eps(x)$ after each step. This PGD-10 is used for training, white-box evaluation and every transfer matrix. AutoAttack~\cite{croce2020reliable} is the standard ensemble of APGD-CE, APGD-T, FAB-T and Square. Two further source attacks probe the dependence on the attack: momentum PGD-10 after MI-FGSM~\cite{dong2018boosting} ($\mu = 1.0$, $\alpha = \eps/10$) and, on ViT sources only, TGR~\cite{zhang2023tgr} (Section~\ref{subsec:rw_transfer}) with momentum $1.0$, $\alpha = 2/255$ and $\gamma = 0.5$. Our TGR applies one $\gamma$ to the attention, QKV and MLP paths and zeroes one maximum and one minimum entry per sample and channel across tokens, so its results speak for this adaptation only.

For attack $k$, $L_k(m) = |P|^{-1}\sum_{c\in P}\log T_k(m\to c)$ is the panel-mean log transfer of source $m$, and $\Gk{k}$ is the gap in $L_k$ between the R32 and ViT-Tiny runs. Each source's best attack is the permitted one with the highest $L_k$, chosen on even-indexed images and scored on odd-indexed ones and vice versa; the retained fraction $\Gk{best}/\Gk{PGD}$ asks whether the gap survives that choice. Step sizes differ between attacks and are not tuned.

\subsection{Adversarial Training}
\label{subsec:training}

% KAYNAK: eski metin 03:78-100 (C1 tarifi, dogrulama bolmesi, tohumlar);
% CLAUDE.md (LR secimi). Tasarim sabitleri, sonuc degil.
All models minimize $\tfrac12\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(f_\theta(x), y) + \tfrac12\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(f_\theta(x_{adv}), y)$, with $x_{adv}$ generated by PGD-10 in training mode as in the reference implementation of Rice et al.~\cite{rice2020overfitting}. Half of the loss is clean and training starts from clean checkpoints, so this mixed-loss PGD adversarial fine-tuning is not the PGD adversarial training of Madry et al.~\cite{madry2018towards}, and its robust accuracies should not be read against reference results for that method. One recipe serves every cell so that architecture is not confounded with the defense.

For R32 and ViT-Tiny, clean training runs 200 epochs with cosine annealing~\cite{loshchilov2016sgdr}, using SGD (learning rate 0.1, weight decay $5\times10^{-4}$) for the CNN and AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2017decoupled} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay 0.05) for the ViT. Adversarial fine-tuning then runs with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and a cosine schedule over up to 100 epochs, with SGD and batch 128 for the CNN and AdamW and batch 64 for the ViT, gradient clipping at $L_2$ norm 10, skipping of non-finite batches, and random crops with 4-pixel padding and horizontal flips. Selection and early stopping (patience 20, threshold 0.1 points) use PGD-10 accuracy on a fixed 2{,}000-image validation split, drawn once with seed 777 and excluded from both training stages of every model. Seeds are 1001 to 1003 for R32 and 2001 to 2003 for ViT-Tiny, paired by index; tables give means and standard deviations over runs.

An earlier pair of checkpoints, selected on a validation split that clean pre-training had already seen, gave the same absolute ordering; it predates the leakage-free protocol, and none of its numbers enters this paper.

\subsection{Transfer Metrics and Conditioning Protocols}
\label{subsec:metrics}

% KAYNAK: eski metin 03:109-116 (bootstrap 10,000; tam test kumesi).
Robust accuracy counts clean-misclassified images as non-robust, so it factorizes exactly as clean accuracy times conditioned survival; the complement of the latter is the white-box conditioned fooling rate $w_j$ of model $j$. For transfer from source $i$ to target $j$, the \emph{target-correct} rate is the fraction of images that $j$ classifies correctly and misclassifies on the example crafted on $i$,
\begin{equation}
    T_{ij} = \frac{\sum_{x} \mathbf{1}[f_j(x) = y]\,\mathbf{1}[f_j(x_{adv}^{(i)}) \neq y]}{\sum_{x} \mathbf{1}[f_j(x) = y]},
\end{equation}
so that $w_j = T_{jj}$. The decomposition uses this rate throughout, because its diagonal is conditioned in the same way. Conditioning on correctly classified images is established practice (Section~\ref{subsec:rw_protocols}), yet the conditioning set is a protocol choice, and the protocol layer (Section~\ref{subsec:transfer}) scores three alternatives on the same per-sample records (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_masks}): the unconditioned rate, the \emph{both-correct} rate~\cite{mahmood2021robustness, ravikumar2023trend} and the \emph{successful-source} rate of Ravikumar et al.~\cite{ravikumar2023trend}, read strictly so that white-box success presupposes a clean-correct source. Rates use full test sets with per-sample outcomes logged; intervals for fixed checkpoints come from a percentile bootstrap over images with 10{,}000 resamples.

Write $e$ for the target's clean error, $r_{\text{raw}}$ and $r_{\text{cond}}$ for the unconditioned and target-correct rates of a direction ($r_{\text{cond}} = T_{ij}$), and $p$ for the probability that a sample the target misclassifies on clean input is also misclassified on the source-crafted input. By the law of total probability,
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{raw}} = e\,p + r_{\text{cond}}\,(1 - e),
\label{eq:general_identity}
\end{equation}
and, when $p=1$,
\begin{equation}
r_{\text{raw}} - r_{\text{cond}} = e\,(1 - r_{\text{cond}}).
\label{eq:raw_identity}
\end{equation}
Over a single model $p=1$ holds by definition, but in transfer a fixed perturbation can return a misclassified sample to the correct class, so $p$ must be measured. Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity} is an accounting identity whose only empirical content is $p$; where $p<1$, Equation~\eqref{eq:raw_identity} overstates the deviation by $e\,(1-p)$. On the scale $h = -\log(1-T)$ the identity also says which conclusions the protocol can change.

\begin{proposition}[Protocol gauge on the $h$ scale]
\label{prop:hscale}
For every direction $i \to j$, $h^{\mathrm{raw}}_{ij} - h^{\mathrm{tc}}_{ij} = -\log(1 - e_j) + g_{ij}$ with $g_{ij} = -\log\bigl(1 + e_j(1-p_{ij})/[(1-e_j)(1-T_{ij})]\bigr)$, which vanishes at $p_{ij} = 1$. Source effects, pairwise symmetric terms and circulation of an additive decomposition of the $h$ matrix are therefore the same under the raw and target-correct protocols up to $g$, and only the target effects shift, by the known amount $-\log(1-e_j)$.
\end{proposition}

This follows from rearranging Equation~\eqref{eq:general_identity} as $1 - r_{\text{raw}} = (1-e)(1-r_{\text{cond}}) + e(1-p)$. The both-correct protocol is no such gauge, since its mask depends on the source's clean accuracy.

\subsection{Vulnerability, Similarity and Direction}
\label{subsec:decomposition_def}

% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 2 (tanimlar, bolunmus yari, logit ve oran olcekleri,
% panel uyesinin kendi degeri); 01_oneri_P3_kuram_olcum.md satir 15-27
% (Onerme 1); brif Sec. 3.1 gosterim.
For two models with target-correct rates $T_{ij}$, $T_{ji}$ and white-box rates $w_i$, $w_j$, define
\begin{align}
\log\simt_{ij} &= \tfrac12\left[\log(T_{ij}T_{ji}) - \log(w_i w_j)\right], \label{eq:tau_def}\\
r_{ij} &= \log(T_{ij}/T_{ji}) - \log(w_j/w_i). \label{eq:r_def}
\end{align}
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
\log T_{ij} = \log w_j + \log\simt_{ij} + \tfrac12 r_{ij},
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
where the similarity $\simt_{ij} = \simt_{ji}$ is shared by both directions and the directional residual $r_{ij} = -r_{ji}$ is the log asymmetry left after the vulnerability ratio is removed. Equation~\eqref{eq:decomp} is an accounting identity; its content lies in the measured sizes of the terms and in how they respond to the design. Without $r$, $T_{ij} = w_j\simt_{ij}$ is the quasi-symmetry model of Caussinus~\cite{caussinus1965contribution} (Section~\ref{subsec:rw_decomposition}) with the column effect fixed at a measured value, close to the split of Demontis et al.~\cite{demontis2019adversarial} into target vulnerability and symmetric alignment (Figure~\ref{fig:decomposition_schematic}).

% Sekil: paper/figures/konsey/fig_decomposition_schematic.py (kanit fig_decomposition_schematic.kanit.json); taslak, visio-cizici turu bekliyor.
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/konsey/fig_decomposition_schematic.pdf}
\caption{Decomposition used throughout. The log target-correct rate splits exactly into the target's vulnerability, a symmetric similarity and half a directional residual. Panel averages of the last two (overline: mean over panel members $c\in P$) give $\sigma$ and $\rho$, which split a source gap between two models into symmetric and directional parts.}
\label{fig:decomposition_schematic}
\end{figure}

Model-level quantities average over a fixed panel $P$ of external models,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\sigma_m = \frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{c\in P}\log\simt_{mc}, \qquad \rho_m = \frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{c\in P} r_{cm},\\
\omega_m = \log w_m.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:panel_def}
\end{equation}
A high $\sigma_m$ means that $m$ shares adversarial directions with the panel; a positive $\rho_m$ means that $m$ receives more from the panel than it sends beyond what vulnerability explains, that is, $m$ is a directionally weak source. For models $R$ and $V$ the panel source gap
\begin{equation*}
G = \frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{c\in P}\left[\log T_{Rc} - \log T_{Vc}\right] = (\sigma_R - \sigma_V) - \tfrac12(\rho_R - \rho_V)
\end{equation*}
has symmetric share $q = (\sigma_R - \sigma_V)/G$, and the panel's own vulnerabilities cancel from it. Within-family similarity $\Dseed$ is the mean $\log\simt$ over pairs of ViT seeds minus the mean of their $\sigma$. The panel is what makes source and target roles identifiable.

\begin{proposition}[Identifiability]
\label{prop:identifiability}
Let $\ell_{ij} = \log T_{ij}$ for $i \neq j$. (a) With two models, the antisymmetric part of $\ell$ is the single number $\ell_{12} - \ell_{21}$, and an additive fit $\ell_{ij} = \mu + \alpha_i + \beta_j + s_{ij}$ with symmetric $s$ identifies only $(\alpha_1 - \alpha_2) - (\beta_1 - \beta_2)$, not its split between source and target. (b) If $\ell_{ij}$ contains a family term $\gamma\,\mathbf{1}[i,j \in C]$ and the design has a single model $V \notin C$, the additive fit absorbs the term into the source and target effects of $V$ with equal weight $-\gamma$, leaving the asymmetry unchanged. (c) Any symmetric term in $\ell_{ij}$, the family term included, cancels from $r_{ij}$ and enters $\log\simt_{ij}$ in full; over a fixed panel it moves $\sigma_m$ and leaves $\rho_m$ unchanged.
\end{proposition}

Part (a) is the degree-of-freedom count of the Hodge decomposition, $K-1$ potential and $(K-1)(K-2)/2$ circulation degrees of freedom for $K$ models~\cite{jiang2011statistical}; (b) follows from $\mathbf{1}[i,j\in C] = 1 - \mathbf{1}[i=V] - \mathbf{1}[j=V]$ for $i\neq j$, and (c) from substitution into Equations~\eqref{eq:tau_def} and~\eqref{eq:r_def}. With one ViT, an additive source effect of the ViT is thus indistinguishable from its dissimilarity to the CNNs, which the decomposition assigns to $\sigma$.

Estimates are split-half: $w$ comes from the even-indexed test images and $T$ from the odd-indexed ones and vice versa, and the halves are averaged, so a diagonal and an off-diagonal cell never share images; $\omega$ uses the full-sample $w$. A panel member's own $\sigma$ and $\rho$ leave that member out. The natural logarithm is primary; as sensitivities, the logit scale replaces $\log x$ by $\log[x/(1-x)]$, and the ratio scale uses the rates themselves.

Its pair is the CIFAR-100 ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny with three seeds each, selected under the single-split rule used when they were trained, which differs from the CIFAR-10 rule; the matrix has 15 models.

On ImageNet the panel holds six CNNs: \texttt{Salman\allowbreak{}2020\allowbreak{}Do\_\allowbreak{}R18}, \texttt{\_\allowbreak{}R50} and \texttt{\_\allowbreak{}50\_\allowbreak{}2}~\cite{salman2020adversarially}, \texttt{Engstrom\allowbreak{}2019\allowbreak{}Robustness}, \texttt{Chen\allowbreak{}2024\allowbreak{}Data\_\allowbreak{}WRN\_\allowbreak{}50\_\allowbreak{}2}~\cite{chen2024data} and \texttt{Peng\allowbreak{}2023\allowbreak{}Robust}~\cite{peng2023robust}. Two CNN-transformer pairs adversarially trained with a shared recipe from clean-trained models, ConvNeXt-T with ViT-S and ConvNeXt-B with ViT-B, all with convolutional stems~\cite{singh2023revisiting}, form the ImageNet-initialized row; $\bar{A}^{\sigma}_{\mathrm{IN}}$ is their mean of $\sigma(\text{transformer}) - \sigma(\text{CNN})$. ConvNeXt-B and Swin-B of Liu et al.~\cite{liu2023comprehensive} give the scratch row, though only indirectly: their shared configuration lacks a pre-training flag. Mo et al.'s ViT-B~\cite{mo2022adversarial} enters unpaired and XCiT-S12 as a secondary member, for 14 models, evaluated on 2{,}000 ImageNetV2 MatchedFrequency images~\cite{recht2019imagenet}, the first two files of each class in lexicographic order. One preprocessing pipeline (bicubic resize to 256, center crop to 224) serves all models, so a source's adversarial tensor reaches every target in the same pixel space; normalization stays as each RobustBench model applies it, and scoring uses all 1{,}000 logits. Each model's clean accuracy on its own pipeline had to reach 25\% and 0.6 times its RobustBench value, within 5 points of the common pipeline. This panel is descriptive and does not replicate the two-by-two design: initialization there means clean pre-training on the same dataset, and the architectures differ.
\end{sloppypar}

% 3.8 Gradient alignment
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Input-Gradient Alignment}
\label{subsec:gradcos_def}

% KAYNAK: EK Sec. 6.1-6.3 (tanim, ham ve ana etki artikli Spearman,
% permutasyon 9999, tohum 20261030, karar kurali).
To ask whether the symmetric term reflects something measured without transfer or attack, we compute for every model pair the mean cosine between input gradients on clean images,
\begin{equation}
c_{ij} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{x} \cos\left(\nabla_x \mathcal{L}_i(x,y),\, \nabla_x \mathcal{L}_j(x,y)\right),
\label{eq:cij}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{L}_m$ the cross-entropy of model $m$, gradients in $[0,1]$ pixel space through any in-model normalization, models in evaluation mode, and the images of the corresponding transfer matrix. This is the image average of the gradient similarity of Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2024why}, and alignment is the symmetric factor of Demontis et al.~\cite{demontis2019adversarial}.

The raw statistic is the Spearman correlation $\rS$ between $c_{ij}$ and $\log\simt_{ij}$ over unordered pairs. The residual statistic correlates the residuals of a least-squares fit $X_{ij} = \mu + a_i + a_j + e_{ij}$ to each matrix, which removes every model-level property that adds to both members of a pair, such as robustness, gradient norm or clean accuracy; it is a partial Mantel test~\cite{smouse1986multiple} covering all main effects at once. Significance comes from the Mantel test~\cite{mantel1967detection}, permuting model labels jointly over the rows and columns of $c$ with $\log\simt$ fixed, 9{,}999 times, for a one-sided $p$ with floor $10^{-4}$. Alignment at one clean point is a necessary condition for similar transfer and not evidence of a mechanism, and the additive fit leaves in place multiplicative or interacting model effects as well as the block structure of the CNN and transformer families.

\subsection{Statistical Inference}
\label{subsec:statistics}

% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 2, 5, 6 (cikarim birimi, delta, karar ilkesi, Holm,
% sonuc haritasi); EK Sec. 2, 4.2, 5.6, 6.3. delta = 0.10 ve 0.06 tasarim
% sabitidir. Dayanaktaki RMS degerleri (havuz r, 21 cift; yalniz CNN
% ciftleri) foyde yok, sayisiz yazildi.
The unit of inference is the training run, with images as repeated measurements shared by all models, so intervals over runs are conditional on the fixed test images and panel members; image sampling enters only the bootstraps that resample images. Two-by-two contrasts use run-level cell means and a pooled-variance $t$ interval with 5 degrees of freedom. Tests form registered families with Holm correction at $\alpha = 0.05$ (Table~\ref{tab:registry}). Equivalence is tested by two one-sided tests (TOST) with margin $\delta = 0.10$ log units, roughly a ten percent relative change in a rate. The margin came from the root-mean-square directional residual among the adversarially trained models seen before registration; that set includes the ViT, so the margin is loose, and every equivalence is repeated at $\delta = 0.06$, the value for CNN pairs alone.

% KAYNAK: ON_KAYIT Sec. 6 satir 100 (O1 bolgesi ve onceden yazilmis manseti).
Support is read from Holm-adjusted $p < 0.05$, refutation and equivalence from raw intervals, with refutation checked first, so a significant effect whose interval lies within $\pm\delta$ counts as negligible. A test that cannot be computed counts as $p = 1$, and predicted sizes are reported without being criteria. A headline was written in advance for every combination of H1 to H3 outcomes, and the masking rule applies after the region is chosen. In region O1, where H1 and H2 are supported and H3 is equivalent, the registered headline, which the results below narrow, attributes the ViT's weak-source appearance to a symmetric, architectural dissimilarity that ImageNet initialization reduces more for the ViT, with the asymmetry carried by target vulnerability and a small directional term independent of architecture. Secondary analyses S1 to S7 are exploratory and uncorrected, and protocol equivalence is tested by TOST at $\pm1$ and $\pm3$ points.
