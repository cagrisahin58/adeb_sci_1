\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Deep neural networks are vulnerable to adversarial examples, carefully crafted perturbations that cause confident misclassification while remaining bounded by a small norm budget~\cite{szegedy2014intriguing, goodfellow2015explaining}. As Vision Transformers (ViTs)~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image, vaswani2017attention} joined convolutional neural networks (CNNs)~\cite{he2016deep} as a dominant architecture family, a natural question followed: which family is more robust, and why?

The literature answers this question inconsistently: Bai et al.~\cite{bai2021transformers} report that under matched training recipes transformers do not surpass CNNs, whereas Benz et al.~\cite{benz2021adversarial} report ViTs to be more robust for standardly trained models. Transfer studies add a disagreement of their own: several report that attacks move more easily from ViTs to CNNs than the reverse~\cite{mahmood2021robustness, benz2021adversarial, shao2022adversarial}, whereas Naseer et al.~\cite{naseer2022improving} find that perturbations crafted on ViTs transfer poorly under conventional attacks, and the direction measured in this paper is the opposite of the first group's, a point Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to. These disagreements are usually attributed to differences in models, training recipes, or datasets, and the standard response is to compare under a matched recipe.

Yet even when the models, the data, the threat model and the attack budget are all held fixed, a transfer rate still depends on a choice that studies state but rarely vary: the \emph{conditioning set}, that is, which samples enter the denominator. The candidates are all test samples, those the target classifies correctly, those both models classify correctly, and those on which the source attack succeeded. Mahmood et al.~\cite{mahmood2021robustness}, Ravikumar et al.~\cite{ravikumar2023trend} and Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2025revisiting} each name the set they use and proceed with it, which is sound practice, but it leaves open how much a different set would have changed the conclusion on the same models. Robustness comparisons face a parallel choice between an absolute score and its separation into clean accuracy and conditional susceptibility.

% Approach
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The setting is kept small so that this question can be isolated. We train ResNet-18 and ViT-Tiny on CIFAR-10~\cite{krizhevsky2009learning} under one matched recipe, clean pre-training followed by mixed-loss PGD adversarial fine-tuning, with three independent training runs per architecture and a fixed validation split held out before \emph{any} training stage, so that no model is selected on data it has seen. All evaluations run on the full test set with per-sample outcome logging, so the same attack outcomes can be rescored under every protocol without re-running an attack. With the models fixed, only the protocol is varied. Two later additions need no training of our own: the clean-trained checkpoints of the same pipeline together with a standard-trained WideResNet-28-10, and an eight-model pool that adds robust checkpoints from RobustBench~\cite{croce2021robustbench}.

Architecture comparisons also invite three readings that we treat as measurable hypotheses: that CNN input gradients are spatially more localized, that adversarial perturbation degrades ViT attention, and that ViT-specific transfer attacks improve cross-architecture transfer. Only the last is a published claim; the first two are interpretations that a sparsity result or a small batch of attention figures readily suggests, our own earlier figures included.

Our first contribution is a measurement of protocol sensitivity on fixed models. Across four conditioning protocols the CNN-to-ViT transfer asymmetry ranges from $+4.36$ to $+19.37$ points, while within any one protocol retraining both architectures moves it across a range of at most $2.41$ points. Three separate facts describe the protocol range, and they do not add up to it. The unconditioned estimate exceeds the target-correct one by $9.21$ points, a gap the target's clean error predicts exactly. The successful-source protocol estimates a different quantity and lies $11.10$ points above the both-correct one. The two protocols we endorse, target-correct and both-correct, differ by $3.91$ points, with a run-level 95\% interval of $[3.12, 4.70]$. Whether an equivalence claim can be made at all also depends on the protocol (Section~\ref{subsec:svhn}).

The second contribution concerns unconditioned rates. Their deviation from the target-correct rate follows from an elementary identity, the transfer analogue of the TRADES decomposition~\cite{zhang2019theoretically}, whose premise we measure: the probability $p$ that a sample the target already misclassifies stays misclassified under a transferred perturbation. On adversarially trained targets $p$ lies between $0.989$ and $1.000$ over $36$ directions, so the correction is exact to within a few tenths of a point. On standard-trained targets $p$ falls to $0.877$ and the $p=1$ form misses the unconditioned rate by up to $1.58$ points, so there the general form, with $p$ measured, has to be used.

The third contribution locates the direction. In $3\times3$ transfer matrices that add a WideResNet-28-10, an additive source-plus-target fit to the target-correct rates assigns the asymmetry to the source: examples crafted on our ViT-Tiny transfer worse to every target, the external one included, while the ViT is the \emph{less} vulnerable target (source terms $+5.13$ and $+9.54$ points on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, target terms $-2.03$ and $-6.19$). Read through raw rates, the same matrices make the ViT look like the weaker target, because raw rates carry its clean error. In the $8\times8$ matrices of Mahmood et al.~\cite{mahmood2021robustness} the source term favors the ViT instead, and their CIFAR-100 pairs that share our direction reach it through the target term.

The fourth contribution is a matched-pair prediction. Because the identity term scales with the clean-accuracy gap, the unconditioned, target-correct and both-correct estimates should converge for pairs matched in clean accuracy, whatever their architectures. They do: on SVHN, where our two models are $1.85$ points apart in clean accuracy, the four-protocol spread is $3.70$ points; for a standard-trained ResNet-18 and WideResNet-28-10 that are $0.47$ points apart it is $0.19$; and across the $28$ pairs of the eight-model pool the three-protocol spread rises by $0.872$ points per point of gap. The successful-source term does not follow the gap. The checkpoint-selection rule moves the clean gap, and with it the spread, by about two points without changing the direction.

Absolute robustness favors the CNN under every attack and factors exactly into clean accuracy and conditional susceptibility; both factors favor the CNN in the present runs, although their attribution differed on an earlier checkpoint set. Three further readings do not survive direct measurement, and Section~\ref{subsec:readings} reports them together: adversarially trained CNN gradients are sparser but not more spatially localized, CLS attention entropy does not change under attack, and a ViT-specific transfer attack gives no gain in this regime. The reporting requirements that follow from these measurements are collected in Section~\ref{subsec:implications}.

A preliminary version of this study was submitted to ATEEC 2026~\cite{sahin2026behavioral}. That version reports the conditional decomposition, the four-protocol transfer comparison, the gradient structure measurements and the block-wise drift profiles on CIFAR-10 alone, and describes them as preliminary. The present paper adds two further datasets, a second threat model, the identity of Section~\ref{subsec:metrics} with its measured premise, the source-target decomposition, standard-trained targets and an external model pool, the analysis of checkpoint selection, the transformer-specific transfer attack and the pre-registered analysis protocol, and it corrects one claim of the earlier version: the direction of the asymmetry is not stable once the architectures are matched in clean accuracy.
